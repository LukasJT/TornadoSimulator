Community Tornado Shelters

Community tornado shelters: public shelter locations, mobile home community programs, and shared shelter access. When to use community shelters.

Types of Community Shelters

Municipal Shelters

Public government-provided:

  • Fire stations
  • Community centers
  • Public libraries
  • Schools (during hours)
  • Churches (may vary)
  • Municipal buildings

Mobile Home Community Shelters

For mobile home residents:

  • Community-owned shelters
  • Manager-provided access
  • Grant-funded installations
  • Growing availability
  • Life-saving option

Business Shelters

Some businesses provide:

  • Public shelter access
  • Employee shelter areas
  • Retail store shelter zones
  • Volunteer public access

Church Shelters

Many churches offer:

  • Basement access
  • Interior corridors
  • Community programs
  • Faith-based support
  • Community members welcome

Why Community Shelters Matter

Population Without Shelters

Many people lack:

  • Personal storm shelters
  • Basements
  • Safe interior rooms
  • Access to shelter at home
  • Community shelters fill gap

Mobile Home Vulnerability

Mobile homes:

  • No structural tornado safety
  • Cannot shelter in place
  • Community shelter essential
  • Life-saving during EF2+ events
  • Growing awareness

Nighttime Warnings

Community shelters:

  • Some are 24-hour access
  • Emergency access
  • Sleep-time preparation
  • Life-saving during nighttime events

Grant-Funded Shelters

Programs providing:

  • Federal FEMA grants

  • Growing availability

Some states require:

  • Community shelter provision

Advocacy Efforts

Push for:

  • Community shelter requirements
  • Funding for programs
  • Public awareness

  • Resident safety

Warning Access

Community Sirens

Outdoor sirens signal:

  • Go inside to shelter
  • Community shelter activation
  • Emergency response
  • Community-wide notification

Individual Notifications

Combined with:

  • NOAA weather radio
  • Wireless Emergency Alerts
  • Local TV/radio
  • Community alert systems
  • Multi-source approach

Community Communication

Some communities have:

  • Neighborhood watch alerts
  • Community text messages
  • Emergency phone trees
  • Social media groups
  • Direct communication

Preparing for Community Shelter Use

Know Locations

Before tornado season:

  • Identify nearest shelter
  • Multiple option locations
  • Route planning
  • Alternate paths
  • Address information

Practice Movement

Family practice:

  • Route familiarity
  • Time estimation
  • Elderly member considerations
  • Child protocols
  • Emergency preparation

Have Supplies Ready

Grab-bag for shelter:

  • NOAA weather radio
  • Cell phone charger
  • Flashlight
  • Emergency contacts
  • Water bottles
  • Comfort items
  • Medications

During Warning Response

Immediate Action

When warning issued:

  1. Move to community shelter immediately
  2. Notify family/friends
  3. Take emergency supplies
  4. Follow shelter procedures
  5. Stay until all-clear

Shelter Etiquette

At community shelter:

  • Follow instructions
  • Cooperate with staff
  • Assist elderly and disabled
  • Remain calm
  • Share space respectfully

Family Coordination

During shelter time:

  • Text family members
  • Stay together if possible
  • Monitor weather
  • Support one another
  • Wait for all-clear

After Tornado Response

Wait for All-Clear

Don't leave shelter:

  • Multiple tornadoes possible
  • Weather may continue
  • Officials will notify
  • Stay until safe

Communication

After event:

  • Contact family
  • Check social media
  • Follow official updates
  • Community connections
  • Support neighbors

Return Home

Only when safe:

  • Verify home is safe
  • Check for damage
  • Watch for downed wires
  • Contact insurance
  • Community assistance

Strong programs:

  • State shelter grants
  • SoonerSafe rebate program
  • Community shelter installations
  • Expanding programs

Post-2011 expansion:

  • State shelter programs
  • Community initiatives
  • Public safety focus
  • Rural coverage

Texas, Kansas, Nebraska

Growing programs:

  • State-supported initiatives
  • Rural community programs
  • Multi-agency coordination

Bottom Line

Community tornado shelters save lives for those without personal shelters. Mobile home communities particularly benefit. Know your community shelter locations before tornado season. Multiple options often available. Practice using them. Advocate for expanded programs. Combined with warning systems and personal preparation, community shelters strengthen overall tornado safety.
