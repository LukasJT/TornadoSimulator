Why do nor’easters form off the East Coast?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

Nor’easters often form near the East Coast because cold continental air sits close to much warmer Atlantic air. An upper-level disturbance can help a low develop along that boundary. The low can then intensify and move northeast as moisture wraps into the circulation.

How it works

The Gulf Stream helps maintain the ocean-side warmth and supplies moisture to air passing over it. The land–ocean temperature contrast supports a front, while an approaching upper trough can help air rise and surface pressure fall. A storm can pull cold air south on its inland side and transport warm, humid air north on its ocean side. Check the surface front and upper-level flow together before interpreting a satellite image.

How did a documented low follow this setup?

The February 2026 Rhode Island storm developed around the Outer Banks on February 22 and deepened offshore before passing southeast of Cape Cod.
