What made the January 2016 nor’easter so disruptive across the Mid-Atlantic?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

The January 2016 Northeast blizzard disrupted highways, airports and transit because heavy snow and high winds hit a densely traveled region. Coastal flooding created an additional hazard near the ocean.

Advertisement

How it works

The NWS service assessment documents the January 22–24 event and reviews how timing affected impacts. In the Mid-Atlantic, some snow arrived faster than forecast and caused travel trouble earlier than expected. On the Pennsylvania Turnpike, the assessment describes significant impacts during the afternoon and examines warnings and reports as conditions deteriorated. Near the coast, wind-driven flooding produced a separate set of disruptions.

What would a travel-focused forecast emphasize?

Track the onset of accumulation and poor visibility along the entire route and check official closures. A correct final snowfall total alone leaves the onset and location of travel impacts unresolved.
