When does a nor’easter qualify as a bomb cyclone?

Calculate the latitude-adjusted pressure drop for bombogenesis and compare it with the verified February 2026 Northeast blizzard.

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

A nor’easter qualifies as an explosively deepening cyclone when its central sea-level pressure falls fast enough over 24 hours. The classic benchmark is 24 hPa at latitude 60°. At lower latitudes the equivalent threshold is smaller.

Calculate a latitude-adjusted threshold

For a 24-hour period at representative latitude φ, compare the pressure fall with 24 × sin(φ) / sin(60°) hPa. At 40° the threshold is about 17.8 hPa. Use the latitude of the cyclone track during the analysis window as specified by the method. A pressure fall at a fixed coastal station measures a different thing from the pressure decrease following the cyclone center.

Apply the test to the February 2026 blizzard

The state record report’s meteorological analysis follows the offshore center from 1,004 to 968 hPa. That is 36 hPa in 24 hours and exceeds either the 40° illustration or the commonly cited 24 hPa benchmark. The pressure analyses document the rate of deepening. Check local warnings and impact observations for the winds, snow or water level that reached a particular community.
