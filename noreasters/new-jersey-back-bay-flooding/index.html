Why does nor’easter flooding linger in New Jersey back bays?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

New Jersey back-bay flooding can linger because water that moves in through inlets and over low terrain takes time to drain. Persistent onshore winds can restrict that drainage through several tidal cycles.

How it works

A bay and an ocean gauge need not peak together. Read both hydrographs in the same local time zone and check each station’s own datum and impact thresholds. Narrow inlets, wind setup along the bay and the timing of successive tides all affect when the bay recedes. An open-coast tide forecast alone can miss that delay.

How would you test whether a bay peak lagged the ocean peak?

Compare the two observed time series for the same event and subtract their peak times. Avoid comparing water heights until datums are reconciled.
