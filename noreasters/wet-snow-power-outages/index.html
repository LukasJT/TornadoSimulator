Why can a nor’easter knock out power with just a few inches of snow?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

Wet snow can cling to branches and wires. A few inches can cause outages if the snow is dense, leaves give it extra surface to collect on, and gusts strain the added load.

How it works

In the late-October 2011 event, NWS Mount Holly documented unusual early snowfall while many trees still had leaves. Wet snow loaded trees and led to widespread damage. Snow depth and snow water equivalent describe different aspects of the load. Two inches of dense snow can contain more water than a deeper layer of dry powder.

Compare two inches of 5:1 snow with four inches of 20:1 snow. Which has more water equivalent?

The dense snow has 0.4 inches of water equivalent. The deeper powder has 0.2 inches.
