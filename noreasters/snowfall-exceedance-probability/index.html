What does a 30% chance of six inches of nor’easter snow mean?

Interpret a probabilistic snowfall threshold, compare two thresholds at one point and keep the forecast time window straight.

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

A 30% chance of six inches of snow means the designated forecast point has a 30% chance of meeting the product’s six-inch threshold during the stated period. Open the legend to check whether the threshold is expressed as “at least” or “greater than.”

Compare thresholds on one clock

In this example, 30% of the modeled outcome distribution meets the six-inch threshold. Each outcome of six inches or more also reaches two inches, so the chance for two inches is at least as high. The forecast gives probabilities for a point and accumulation window. Recheck both when moving the map or switching days.

Estimate the probability between two thresholds

Using the table, the chance of at least 2 inches and less than 6 inches is 70% − 30% = 40%. The chance of less than 6 inches is 70%. Neither arithmetic step implies an expected accumulation of 1.8 inches, because many totals could occur above or below six inches.

Find your product

Open the NWS probabilistic snowfall page or your local forecast office, choose the threshold, and read the issue time and valid interval. If a product gives a 48-hour accumulation, it cannot isolate the portion that falls before an individual commute.
