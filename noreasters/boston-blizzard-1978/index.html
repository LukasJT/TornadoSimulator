Why did the Blizzard of 1978 strand drivers around Boston?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

In February 1978, heavy snow and wind reduced visibility on Boston-area highways while many drivers were still on the road. Once vehicles became stuck, that blockage compounded the snowfall by making passage and plowing harder.

How it works

The National Weather Service Boston retrospective lists about 3,500 stranded cars along Route 128 and shows the abandoned vehicles in its archival material. The interior transportation hazard involved low visibility and immobilized traffic. Nearby coastal communities also faced waves and repeated coastal flooding. The same cyclone produced these distinct problems depending on where people were.

Why can a road remain blocked after the snow rate drops?

Stranded vehicles and deep drifts can obstruct lanes even after visibility improves. Clearing the corridor requires access, recovery and plowing.
