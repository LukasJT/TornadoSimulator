What did observers actually measure in the Great Blizzard of 1888?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

Albany’s National Weather Service office lists 46.7 inches of snowfall during the Blizzard of 1888. Drifts were higher because wind moved snow after it fell.

Advertisement

How it works

The storm dates in Albany’s historical summary are March 11–14, 1888. The office lists 46.7 inches as an Albany storm total. A historical storm total combines accumulation over the full event and belongs to the named observing location. It does not give a calendar-day maximum or the deepest snowbank. Early observations also have fewer nearby stations for comparison than modern snowfall surveys.

How should you compare Albany with another city?

Use the same observation type and an overlapping event window. A drift measurement in one community cannot be ranked against the storm snowfall at an observing station.
