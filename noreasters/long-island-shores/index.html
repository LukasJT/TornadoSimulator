What does a nor’easter mean for Long Island’s north and south shores?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

Long Island’s south shore faces the open Atlantic and its north shore faces Long Island Sound. The same nor’easter can produce different wave, water level and flooding impacts on the two sides.

Advertisement

How it works

Determine wind direction relative to each shoreline. Use Atlantic stations and forecasts for ocean exposure, and Sound stations for the north shore. A south-shore barrier and its back bays can have different peak times. Use the NWS New York event archive and NOAA station records to check which coast reached local flood criteria in a particular storm.

Which shore is always hit harder?

There is no fixed ordering. Track, wind, tide phase and local shoreline geometry determine where impacts are greatest.
