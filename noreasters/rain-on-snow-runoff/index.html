Can a rainy nor’easter melt an existing snowpack and raise river levels?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

A rainy nor’easter can generate runoff by adding rain and melting existing snow. The river response depends on snowpack temperatures, rainfall, soil conditions, storage and drainage.

Advertisement

How it works

Estimate the rain and possible melt separately. Existing snow water equivalent sets an upper bound on snow water available to melt. A cold snowpack can first retain or refreeze some water. Compare upstream river gauges and the timing of rain and melt before attributing the river rise.

Does 35 mm gross input mean all 35 mm immediately reaches the river?

No. Some can infiltrate or remain in temporary storage, and runoff takes time to route through the basin.
