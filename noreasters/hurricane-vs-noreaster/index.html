How is a nor’easter different from a hurricane on a weather map?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

A nor’easter typically has fronts and feeds on horizontal temperature contrasts. A mature tropical hurricane organizes around a deep warm core and usually lacks surface fronts. Both can flood the coast and produce dangerous wind.

How it works

Begin with the surface map. A front approaching or wrapping around the low points toward a frontal cyclone. Look next at temperatures aloft: a tropical warm core has comparatively warm air over its center through much of the troposphere. A tropical system can acquire fronts and spread its strongest winds over a broader area during extratropical transition. Use the issuing agency’s latest analysis for a transitioning storm.

Apply the map clues

The February 2026 offshore cyclone’s analyzed fronts extend away from a deep surface low. Compare the surface chart in the committee report with a hypothetical tropical cyclone that has a warm core and closed circulation without fronts. An eye-like satellite feature alone cannot establish cyclone type.
