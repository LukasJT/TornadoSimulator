How does a nor’easter flood the coast at high tide?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

A nor’easter floods the coast when storm-driven water combines with the tide and waves to push water above local thresholds. The greatest flooding can occur around high tide even if the largest storm residual happens earlier.

How it works

At a NOAA gauge, compare observed and predicted water levels at the same time and in the same vertical datum. Their difference is the residual, which can include storm and other non-tidal effects. Flooding depends on the observed total water level, while wave runup can extend impacts above the still-water gauge level.

Which time has the higher total water level?

The later high tide reaches 7.0 ft despite having a smaller residual. Compare 7.0 ft with the gauge’s local thresholds.
