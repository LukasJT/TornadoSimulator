How did the March 1993 Superstorm bring snow and severe weather at once?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

The March 1993 Superstorm brought snow and severe thunderstorms at once because different sectors of its circulation contained different air masses. Cold air and sustained ascent favored snow inland, while warm moist flow supported severe storms farther south.

How it works

Follow the March 12–14 NWS surface analyses. On the cold side, moisture and lift spread heavy snowfall along parts of the Appalachians. In the warm sector, organized convection brought damaging winds and tornadoes. NWS Wilmington lists 11 confirmed tornadoes across Florida. Use the storm-relative sector and observation time when comparing reports.

Would a warm-sector surface report rule out blizzard conditions elsewhere in the same cyclone?

No. The fronts separated air masses across a broad circulation. A single report describes one location and time.
