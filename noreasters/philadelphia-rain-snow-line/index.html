Why does the Philadelphia rain–snow line shift along I-95?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

The Philadelphia rain–snow line shifts as coastal air, the inland cold layer and storm-driven cooling compete. A small track change can move the melting layer across the I-95 corridor.

How it works

Compare the surface and low-level temperature profiles from southeast of the city through its northwestern suburbs. In the October 2011 event, the NWS Mount Holly case shows a sharp late-season-style contrast during an early snowstorm. Inland areas and higher terrain were favored for snow in the marginal air mass. Could a spot near I-95 briefly get snow even if rain is the eventual dominant type?

Yes. Strong precipitation and cooling can briefly make a marginal column cold enough for flakes to reach the surface.
