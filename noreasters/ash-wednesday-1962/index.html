Why did the Ash Wednesday nor’easter of 1962 flood Delaware for days?

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

Delaware flooded repeatedly because the Ash Wednesday storm lingered offshore through five high tides. Persistent onshore winds and waves kept driving water against the coast between successive tide peaks.

How it works

According to NWS Delaware flood history, the nor’easter reached Bethany Beach on the evening of March 5, 1962, with winds reported at 80 mph. The storm damaged beachfront buildings and the boardwalk. Floodwater reached inland as far as Ocean View. The storm’s duration helps explain why impacts continued even when a given tide fell. The flood history identifies the five high tides without giving a complete gauge hydrograph.

Why can the fifth high tide still cause severe flooding?

If onshore winds and waves persist, total water can remain elevated on each successive tide. Erosion and damage from earlier cycles also affect later exposure.
