When Is Tornado Season in Nevada?

Nevada tornado season by month, based on 96 SPC records from 1962 through 2023. See the peak and year-round safety context.

Quick answer
June is the leading month in Nevada’s historical record, but “tornado season” is a peak rather than a boundary. Tornadoes have been documented outside the busiest period.

The peak in Nevada

June accounts for 22 of the state’s 96 archived tornado records. The surrounding months may also be active, and a quiet peak month in one year does not change the long-term pattern.

Seasonality reflects the shifting overlap of warm, humid air, storm-producing lift, and winds that change speed or direction with height.

The quieter part of the calendar

December has the fewest records in this archive, with 0. A lower climatological count means lower frequency, not zero risk.

Cool-season tornadoes can be fast-moving and may occur after dark. Keep alerts enabled throughout the year rather than switching them on only in the peak month.

Prepare before the active months

Choose a shelter, test alerts, fit helmets, update medication and pet supplies, and practice a household drill before the climatological peak.

On any specific day, use the Storm Prediction Center outlook and local National Weather Service forecast. Historical season charts cannot identify today’s risk.

Frequently asked questions

What month has the most tornadoes in Nevada?

June has the largest count in the SPC archive used here, with 22 recorded tornadoes. Individual years can differ sharply.

How many tornadoes have been recorded in Nevada?

The archive contains 96 records from 1962 through 2023. The total reflects reporting and database practices as well as weather.

What is the strongest tornado recorded in Nevada?

The highest rating in this archive is F/EF1. Fujita and Enhanced Fujita ratings are damage-based and are not directly interchangeable wind measurements.

Data source and limits

Counts on this page come from the NOAA Storm Prediction Center historical tornado archive for 1962–2023. Older records are less complete, weaker tornado reporting increased with population and technology, and ratings changed from the Fujita scale to the Enhanced Fujita scale in 2007. A record is not a forecast.
