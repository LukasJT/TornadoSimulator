Tornadoes in Dallas

The DFW metroplex is the largest urban area in Tornado Alley — 8 million people living where violent tornadoes have repeatedly struck. Here is Dallas's tornado history and what residents should know.

The local risk

  • North Texas sits at the southern anchor of Tornado Alley.
  • The 2000 Fort Worth F2 hit downtown directly, shattering skyscraper glass.
  • The 2019 Dallas EF3 tracked through the northern city at night — $1.5B damage, zero deaths.
  • The metroplex's sheer size means some part of it is under a warning nearly every spring.
  • Peak months: April-May, with a smaller fall season.

Notable events

  • April 2, 1957 Dallas F3 — 10 dead; famously photographed and filmed, advancing tornado science.
  • March 28, 2000 Fort Worth F2 — direct downtown hit, 3 dead.
  • May 15, 2013 Granbury EF4 — 6 dead, southwest of the metro.
  • December 26, 2015 Garland-Rowlett EF4 — 10 dead on I-30 during the Boxing Day outbreak.
  • October 20, 2019 Dallas EF3 — nighttime track through north Dallas, $1.5B damage, zero deaths.

How warnings reach you here

  • The local NWS office issues tornado watches and warnings for the metro.
  • Wireless Emergency Alerts push warnings to every phone in the polygon — no signup needed.
  • Outdoor sirens cover most of the metro but are designed for people outdoors.
  • NOAA Weather Radio with SAME county programming is the most reliable overnight alert.
  • Local TV meteorologists provide wall-to-wall coverage during outbreaks.

Preparedness for this area

  1. Few DFW homes have basements — identify your interior lowest-floor room now.
  2. The 2015 Garland deaths were mostly people in vehicles on the highway — do not drive during warnings.
  3. December tornadoes are real here (2015 Boxing Day) — winter warnings deserve full response.
  4. High-rise dwellers: interior stairwell on a low floor, away from glass.
  5. The 2019 EF3 killed nobody at night in a huge city — WEA compliance works; keep alerts on.
