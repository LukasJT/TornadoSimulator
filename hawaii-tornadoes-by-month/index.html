Read the monthly chart

Hawaii’s distribution is built from 41 records spanning 1955–2015. March ranks first and July ranks last in raw count.

Because the period covers many decades, the chart smooths out unusually active and quiet years. It cannot show whether a particular upcoming month will follow the average.

Why monthly counts change

The position of the jet stream, moisture return, frontal boundaries, storm tracks, and local terrain all help shape seasonal opportunities for rotating thunderstorms. Those ingredients do not arrive on a fixed schedule.

Population growth, radar coverage, survey practices, and public reporting also affect the database, especially for brief and weak tornadoes.

Use climatology with current forecasts

Climatology answers when tornadoes have been more common. An outlook answers where severe storms may develop in the coming days, and a warning identifies an immediate threat.

Use each product for its intended timescale. If your location enters a warning polygon, shelter regardless of whether the date falls inside the traditional season.

Frequently asked questions

What month has the most tornadoes in Hawaii?

March has the largest count in the SPC archive used here, with 11 recorded tornadoes. Individual years can differ sharply.

How many tornadoes have been recorded in Hawaii?

The archive contains 41 records from 1955 through 2015. The total reflects reporting and database practices as well as weather.

What is the strongest tornado recorded in Hawaii?

The highest rating in this archive is F/EF2. Fujita and Enhanced Fujita ratings are damage-based and are not directly interchangeable wind measurements.

Data source and limits

Counts on this page come from the NOAA Storm Prediction Center historical tornado archive for 1955–2015. Older records are less complete, weaker tornado reporting increased with population and technology, and ratings changed from the Fujita scale to the Enhanced Fujita scale in 2007. A record is not a forecast.
