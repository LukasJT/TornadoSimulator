What is a nor’easter?

Learn how nor’easters form off the East Coast, why they bring rain or snow, and how to check local wind, travel and coastal flooding impacts.

Tornado Hub · November 21, 2026

A nor’easter is a coastal storm along the Atlantic side of North America named for winds that often blow from the northeast along the coast. Nor’easters are moving low-pressure systems. Their track and the air over each community determine whether that community gets snow, rain, strong wind or coastal flooding.

Advertisement

Why do they form near the coast?

Cold air over land can sit near much warmer air over the western Atlantic. This horizontal contrast helps provide energy for an extratropical cyclone, especially when an approaching upper-level disturbance helps the surface pressure fall. The circulation around the low can push northeast winds onto the coast while carrying moisture inland.

Why might nearby towns get rain and snow?

Temperatures close to the ground and through the clouds determine the precipitation type. Onshore winds can sustain rain near the water while cold air inland supports snow. The heaviest snow may fall in narrow bands. Persistent onshore winds can also raise water levels across more than one high tide.
