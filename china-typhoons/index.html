China Typhoons

Every year, several typhoons slam into China's south-east coast, bringing destructive wind, storm surge, and torrential rain from Guangdong to Zhejiang and beyond. Here is how China's typhoon season works.

At a glance

SeasonMay–November (peak Jul–Sep)
Most-hit coastGuangdong, Fujian, Zhejiang, Hainan
BasinWestern North Pacific — the world's most active
National serviceChina Meteorological Administration (CMA)

The world's busiest typhoon coast

China borders the western North Pacific, the most active tropical-cyclone basin on Earth. Each year several typhoons make landfall along the south-east coast, most often in Guangdong, Fujian, Zhejiang, and Hainan, with impacts reaching far inland as heavy rain. The season runs roughly May to November and peaks from July to September.

Notable storms

Recent history includes Typhoon Saomai (2006), one of the strongest to hit the mainland in decades; Lekima (2019), which caused widespread flooding and many deaths; Mangkhut (2018), which battered Guangdong after devastating the Philippines; and Doksuri (2023), whose remnant rains triggered catastrophic flooding around Beijing and Hebei far from the coast.

The main dangers

Typhoons threaten the coast with destructive wind and storm surge, but their deadliest impact is often inland flooding: slow-moving or decaying typhoons can dump enormous rainfall over river basins and cities hundreds of kilometres from where they came ashore. The CMA's colour-coded typhoon warnings (blue–yellow–orange–red) signal the escalating threat.
