The London (Kensal Rise) Tornado of 2006

TORRO T4 · Kensal Rise, London · December 7, 2006 · No serious injuries

On the morning of December 7, 2006, a tornado touched down in the Kensal Rise and Ladbroke Grove area of northwest London, tearing roofs off around 100 homes and overturning cars along a short but intense track through one of the most densely populated cities in the world.

What Happened

The tornado formed from a vigorous line of showers moving across London and touched down in Kensal Rise, tracking southeast through Ladbroke Grove over a path roughly a mile long. TORRO rated the tornado T4 — roughly comparable to a strong F1 tornado on the Fujita scale — based on the roof and structural damage it left behind.

Because it struck during the morning in a major city full of camera phones and camcorders, the London tornado became one of the most extensively filmed tornadoes in UK history, with numerous resident videos capturing debris flying and roofing material being torn away in real time.

Damage and Impact

Around 100 homes across Kensal Rise and Ladbroke Grove sustained damage, mostly to roofs, chimneys, and garden structures, along with several damaged and overturned vehicles. Remarkably, despite tracking directly through densely packed residential streets during the morning, no serious injuries were reported.

  • Around 100 homes damaged, primarily roofs and chimneys
  • Multiple vehicles damaged or overturned
  • No serious injuries despite a direct hit on a dense urban area
  • Extensively documented on video by residents, unusual for a UK tornado at the time

A Reminder That Tornadoes Can Hit Any City

The London tornado is frequently cited in UK severe weather education as proof that even a global capital city is not immune to tornadoes. While the UK's tornadoes are almost always far weaker than those recorded in the central United States, the 2006 London event demonstrated that a T4-strength tornado can strike a major metropolitan area and cause real, if not deadly, damage.

UK Urban Tornado Context

The London tornado sits alongside the Birmingham tornado of 2005 as one of the two most significant urban tornado events in recent UK history. Both illustrate a similar pattern: modest tornado intensity by international standards, but real, sometimes extensive property damage when a tornado tracks directly through dense residential streets, and — in both cases — a fortunate absence of serious casualties.

Aftermath

Affected homeowners in Kensal Rise and Ladbroke Grove worked with insurers over the following months to repair roofing and structural damage. The event remains a frequently referenced case study — and source of dramatic footage — in UK severe weather and tornado education.
