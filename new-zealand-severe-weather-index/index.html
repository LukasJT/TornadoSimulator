New Zealand's severe weather risk is dominated by ex-tropical cyclones sweeping down from the Pacific, extreme windstorms shaped by its position in the Roaring Forties, and flooding across its steep, erosion-prone hill country — with tornadoes an occasional, and occasionally deadly, surprise. This index tracks New Zealand's most significant and record-breaking severe weather disasters, with full write-ups for the most notable ones.

Record-Breaking Events

The Wahine Disaster

· April 10, 1968

An extreme storm with gusts around 275 km/h sank the inter-island ferry TEV Wahine at the entrance to Wellington Harbour, killing 51 — New Zealand's worst peacetime maritime disaster. Read the full story →

The Auckland Tornado

· May 3, 2011

A tornado struck the Albany area of Auckland's North Shore, killing 3 people in one of the deadliest tornadoes in New Zealand's recorded history. Read the full story →

Cyclone Bola

· March 1988

A slow-moving ex-tropical cyclone stalled over the North Island, dumping around 900mm of rain and triggering catastrophic landslides across the East Cape and Gisborne. Read the full story →

Cyclone Gabrielle

· February 2023

A catastrophic ex-tropical cyclone killed 11 people and triggered only the third national state of emergency in New Zealand's history, devastating Hawke's Bay. Read the full story →

Other Notable Events

  • Auckland Anniversary floods (late January 2023) — record-breaking rainfall flooded Auckland just weeks before Cyclone Gabrielle struck, killing 4 people. Read the full story →
  • The Great Snow (August 2011) — an exceptionally powerful Antarctic outbreak brought Wellington's heaviest snow in decades and rare flurries as far north as greater Auckland. Read the full story →
  • Cyclone Giselle (April 1968) — the tropical remnant that merged with a southerly storm to produce the Wahine disaster.
  • Tasman Tempest / ex-Cyclone Fehi and Gita (February 2018) — back-to-back ex-tropical systems caused significant coastal flooding across the South Island within the same fortnight.
  • Cyclone Drena (January 1997) — brought destructive flooding to the Bay of Plenty and Coromandel during the height of summer.
  • The Wahine Storm's wind record — the ~275 km/h gust recorded above Wellington in April 1968 remains among the strongest ever measured in New Zealand.

Key Facts

  • New Zealand records tornadoes most years — its geography makes it one of the more tornado-prone countries outside classic "Tornado Alley" regions — but most are weak; the 2011 Auckland tornado is a rare, tragic exception.
  • Cyclone Gabrielle in 2023 triggered only the third national state of emergency ever declared in New Zealand, reflecting the geographic scale of the flooding and infrastructure damage.
  • Cyclone Bola (1988) remained the historical benchmark for East Cape/Gisborne flooding for 35 years, until Cyclone Gabrielle's 2023 damage was assessed as comparable or worse in parts of the region.
  • The Wahine storm's recorded gust of roughly 275 km/h remains among the strongest wind readings ever documented in New Zealand.
