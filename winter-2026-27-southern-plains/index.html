Southern Plains Winter 2026–27: Rain, Drought, and Ice

The 2026–27 winter pattern for Texas, Oklahoma, and the southern Plains: NOAA wet odds, drought improvement, and event-scale icing risks.

Published 2026-10-17; based on outlooks available October 17, 2026.

At a glance: NOAA tilts the southern Plains wetter for the season and favors drought improvement in parts of the region. It cannot predict whether a particular storm will be rain, sleet, or freezing rain.

Read the national 2026–27 winter outlook for the full regional context.

A wetter seasonal backdrop

NOAA's October winter outlook favors above-average precipitation across the southern tier from the Southwest toward the Southeast. Parts of the Southern Great Plains are also in the drought-improvement area of the outlook through January. A wet tilt can be helpful for water supplies, but the amount and distribution of actual rain will matter.

The northern Plains have less of a precipitation signal in the same outlook; some areas carry equal chances for each category. Texas and Oklahoma also span varied climates, so a single state-level statement can hide important local differences.

Why winter precipitation type is hard

An active southern storm track does not guarantee snow. A shallow surface cold layer can produce freezing rain while warmer air moves above it, and a small shift in temperature can change an icy road forecast to rain. The October seasonal outlook contains none of the vertical temperature detail needed for that decision.

Heavy rain is also possible during a wet winter pattern. In drought areas, dry or compacted ground may not absorb intense runoff quickly. Local flood watches and road information are the right tools during a developing event.

Preparation that fits the uncertainty

Plan for two distinct winter disruptions: heavy rain and isolated ice or snow episodes. Keep a reliable warning source, check the NWS forecast before long drives, and watch ice probabilities when surface temperatures approach freezing. The seasonal outlook should guide readiness, not a firm travel cancellation.

Recheck CPC updates after November and compare them with observed precipitation and drought status; the October map is an initial signal.
