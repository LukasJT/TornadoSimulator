Tornadoes in Topeka: History, Risk & Safety

On 8 June 1966, an F5 tornado crossed Burnett's Mound and drove straight through Topeka, killing at least 16 people and becoming, at the time, one of the costliest tornadoes in US history. Here is the Kansas capital's tornado story.

The tornado risk in Topeka

As the capital of Kansas, Topeka sits squarely in the traditional heart of Tornado Alley. Late spring brings the reliable overlap of Gulf moisture, instability, and wind shear that fuels violent supercells across the region. Local folklore had long held that nearby Burnett's Mound would protect the city — a myth the 1966 tornado shattered.

Notable tornadoes in Topeka history

8 June 1966 — the F5 that crossed Burnett's Mound

A violent F5 tornado climbed over Burnett's Mound on the city's southwest edge and tore a diagonal path across Topeka, including Washburn University and the downtown area. It killed at least 16 people, injured hundreds, and caused damage that ranked it among the costliest tornadoes on record at the time. The storm ended the local legend that the mound shielded the city from tornadoes.

A tornado-prone capital

Northeast Kansas records tornadoes nearly every spring. Most are weak, but Topeka's position in Tornado Alley means the potential for strong, long-track tornadoes is ever-present through the season.

How tornado warnings reach Topeka

Warnings for the Topeka area are issued by the local National Weather Service forecast office and pushed through Wireless Emergency Alerts to mobile phones, NOAA Weather Radio, local TV and radio, and outdoor sirens where they exist. A tornado watch means conditions are favourable across a broad region; a tornado warning means one is imminent or occurring nearby and you should shelter immediately. Learn the difference on our watch versus warning guide.

Staying safe in Topeka

Know your safe place before the season starts: a basement or storm shelter is best, otherwise a small interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows. If you live in a mobile home, identify a sturdier building nearby, because manufactured homes offer little protection. Keep a NOAA Weather Radio or phone alerts on during severe-weather setups, and never try to outrun a tornado in a vehicle in congested areas. See the full tornado safety guide.

Topeka in the bigger picture

The Topeka tornado is a classic example of how dangerous local myths about terrain 'protecting' a town can be — no hill, river, or city reliably stops a tornado. See more myths debunked in our tornado myths guide and the wider Kansas tornado history.
