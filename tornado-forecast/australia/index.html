Where to find today's official Australian severe-thunderstorm and tornado warnings, how tornadoes form here, and which states see them most.

Today's official Australian outlook

The Bureau of Meteorology (BoM) is the single authoritative source for Australian severe weather. It issues severe-thunderstorm warnings — which explicitly include tornadoes — in real time, state by state. These live pages are always current:

BoM Warnings → The national map of active warnings, including severe thunderstorm warnings that cover tornado, damaging wind, large hail, and flash-flood threats. MetEye → The Bureau's interactive forecast tool — thunderstorm risk, wind, and rain for any location in Australia. Weather radar → Live BoM radar to watch storms develop and track them toward you. Australia storm guide → Our background guide to Australian severe thunderstorms, hail, damaging wind, and how to read BoM warnings.

Tip: install the BoM Weather app and enable warnings for your area. A severe-thunderstorm warning can include a tornado — take it seriously.

Does Australia get tornadoes?

Yes. Australia averages dozens of tornadoes a year, and likely many more that go unrecorded across its vast, thinly populated interior. Most are weak, but the country has produced genuinely violent tornadoes, and waterspouts are common off the coasts. They form the same way as anywhere — thunderstorms with strong instability and wind shear — most often from spring through autumn.

Where tornadoes strike in Australia

RegionNotes
Eastern seaboard (NSW, SE Queensland, Victoria)The most tornado-prone zone, with the densest population and best reporting. Sydney and the NSW coast see repeated events.
Southwest Western AustraliaA recognised tornado corridor, especially in the cooler months when cold fronts sweep through.
Coastal watersFrequent waterspouts around the whole coastline, occasionally moving onshore.

The 1970 Bulahdelah tornado in NSW carved one of the longest tracks on record in Australia. Read more in our Australia severe weather guide and the worldwide tornado guide.

Staying safe

During the storm season keep BoM warnings on, and when a severe-thunderstorm or tornado warning is issued, move indoors to the strongest part of a sturdy building, away from windows. See our tornado safety guide.
