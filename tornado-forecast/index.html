How to read a tornado outlook

Most agencies rate the day's severe-weather threat on a graded scale. In the US the SPC uses five levels — Marginal, Slight, Enhanced, Moderate, and High — plus a general-thunderstorm category, and separate maps for tornado, wind, and hail probability. A higher level means a greater coverage and intensity of severe storms, not a certainty for any one place. Full explanations are on our US forecast page.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I see today's tornado forecast?

For the US, the live SPC map at the top of this page. For other countries, use the official source in the worldwide index — each is that nation's authoritative weather service.

Does this update automatically?

Yes. The US map reloads the latest SPC version through the day, and the official sources in the index are always current — nothing here has to be updated by hand.

What should I do during a tornado warning?

Take shelter immediately in a basement or a small interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows. See our tornado safety guide and watch vs warning explainer.

This is not an official warning source. The US map is embedded live from the NOAA Storm Prediction Center and may lag the latest update; all other sources are external agencies. During active weather, always follow your national weather service, a weather radio, and local emergency alerts. A tornado warning means take shelter now.
