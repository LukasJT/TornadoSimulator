🇪🇺 Europe severe weather

Europe Tornado Forecast

Where to find today's official European severe-storm and tornado forecast, how tornadoes form across the continent, and which countries see them most.

Today's official European outlook

Europe has no single government tornado map, but two authoritative continent-wide sources are updated daily and freely available. Use these as your live starting point:

ESTOFEX → The European Storm Forecast Experiment issues a daily map-based severe-weather forecast for the whole continent, highlighting the tornado, wind, and large-hail threat with a level 1–3 system. Meteoalarm → The official aggregator of severe-weather warnings from every national European weather service — colour-coded thunderstorm and wind alerts by region. ESWD → The European Severe Weather Database from the ESSL — real-time reports of tornadoes, large hail, and damaging wind as they are confirmed across Europe. TORRO (UK) → The Tornado and Storm Research Organisation, which forecasts and catalogues tornadoes across the United Kingdom and Ireland.

National services carry the official warnings for your country — the UK Met Office, Germany's DWD, and Météo-France among them.

Does Europe get tornadoes?

Yes — far more than most people realise. Europe records on the order of several hundred tornadoes a year, including waterspouts, though the great majority are weak and short-lived compared with the violent tornadoes of the US Plains. They form the same way: thunderstorms with enough instability and wind shear, most often in spring and summer, and in autumn along the Mediterranean coasts.

Where tornadoes strike in Europe

RegionNotes
United Kingdom & IrelandAmong the highest tornado frequency per unit area in the world, though nearly all are weak. TORRO tracks them.
Central Europe (Germany, Poland, Czechia)Summer supercells can produce strong tornadoes; the 2021 Czech EF4 was among Europe's most destructive.
France & the Low CountriesRegular weak-to-moderate tornadoes, plus occasional stronger events in the north.
Italy & the MediterraneanFrequent waterspouts and autumn coastal tornadoes, especially around the Adriatic and Tyrrhenian seas.

Staying safe

Follow your national weather service and Meteoalarm during the storm season, and treat a severe-thunderstorm or tornado warning seriously: move indoors to a sturdy building, away from windows, on the lowest floor. See our tornado safety guide.

This is a guide, not a warning source. For live European warnings use Meteoalarm and your national weather service; for the daily storm forecast use ESTOFEX. This page is educational and not affiliated with any of them.
