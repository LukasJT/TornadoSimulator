🇨🇦 Canada severe weather

Canada Tornado Forecast

Where to find today's official Canadian tornado and severe-thunderstorm watches, how Environment and Climate Change Canada forecasts tornadoes, and where the risk is highest across the country.

Today's official Canadian outlook

Canada does not publish a single US-style national tornado-outlook map. Instead, Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC) issues severe-thunderstorm and tornado watches and warnings region by region, in real time. These are the authoritative live sources — always current, no waiting:

ECCC Warnings Map → The official national map of active watches and warnings, including tornado and severe-thunderstorm watches. Your primary live source. Weather by region → Forecasts and alerts for any Canadian city or region, including today's thunderstorm risk. Weather radar → Live ECCC radar to track storms as they develop and move. Northern Tornadoes Project → Western University's project that documents and verifies every Canadian tornado — the country's most detailed tornado record.

Tip: bookmark the ECCC Warnings Map during storm season. A tornado watch means conditions are favourable; a tornado warning means one is imminent or occurring — take shelter now.

How Canada forecasts tornadoes

ECCC meteorologists at regional Storm Prediction Centres watch the same ingredients that drive tornadoes anywhere — warm, moist low-level air, cold air aloft, instability, and wind shear. When those line up, they issue a severe thunderstorm watch and, if rotation is detected on radar or spotted on the ground, a tornado warning for the specific area. Canada also relies heavily on public storm reports and, since 2017, the Northern Tornadoes Project, which uses satellite and drone surveys to confirm tornadoes in remote areas that once went unrecorded — revealing that Canada gets far more tornadoes than long assumed.

Where tornadoes strike in Canada

Canada is the second most tornado-prone country in the world after the United States, with well over 60 confirmed tornadoes in an average year. The risk concentrates in two corridors:

RegionSeasonNotes
Southern Prairies (Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba)June–AugustThe Canadian heart of Tornado Alley; site of the F5 Elie, Manitoba tornado of 2007.
Southern Ontario & QuebecMay–SeptemberDensely populated and frequently hit; the deadly Barrie 1985 and Edmonton-style events.
Southern Quebec & AtlanticSummerLess frequent but not immune, including the 2018 Ottawa–Gatineau outbreak.

Staying safe in Canada

Keep alerts on during the summer storm season — the free ECCC WeatherCAN app and Alert Ready send tornado warnings straight to your phone. When a warning is issued, shelter in a basement or a small interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows. See our full tornado safety guide.

This is a guide, not a warning source. For live Canadian watches and warnings always use Environment and Climate Change Canada (weather.gc.ca) and Alert Ready. This page is educational and is not affiliated with ECCC.
