Tornadoes in Greensboro: History, Risk & Safety

On 15 April 2018, a strong tornado tore through the heart of Greensboro, damaging hundreds of buildings and reminding the Piedmont that its tornado threat is real. Here is the city's tornado history, risk, and safety picture.

The tornado risk in Greensboro

Greensboro sits in North Carolina's Piedmont Triad, where tornadoes are less frequent than on the Plains but can still be strong and destructive. The threat comes from spring severe-weather systems, cold-season storms, and tornadoes spun up by landfalling tropical systems. Because significant tornadoes are relatively uncommon here, they can arrive with little community memory of the last one.

Notable tornadoes in Greensboro history

15 April 2018 — the tornado through the city

A strong tornado moved directly across Greensboro during an April outbreak, killing a resident, injuring others, and damaging or destroying hundreds of homes, businesses, and a school. It was one of the most significant tornadoes to strike the city in modern times and prompted a major recovery effort across the east side.

A Piedmont pattern

The Triad region has recorded numerous tornadoes over the years, most of them weak. The 2018 event showed that, while rare, a strong tornado through a populated part of Greensboro is entirely possible.

How tornado warnings reach Greensboro

Warnings for the Greensboro area are issued by the local National Weather Service forecast office and pushed through Wireless Emergency Alerts to mobile phones, NOAA Weather Radio, local TV and radio, and outdoor sirens where they exist. A tornado watch means conditions are favourable across a broad region; a tornado warning means one is imminent or occurring nearby and you should shelter immediately. Learn the difference on our watch versus warning guide.

Staying safe in Greensboro

Know your safe place before the season starts: a basement or storm shelter is best, otherwise a small interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows. If you live in a mobile home, identify a sturdier building nearby, because manufactured homes offer little protection. Keep a NOAA Weather Radio or phone alerts on during severe-weather setups, and never try to outrun a tornado in a vehicle in congested areas. See the full tornado safety guide.

Greensboro in the bigger picture
