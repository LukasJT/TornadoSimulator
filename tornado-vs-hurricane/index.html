Tornado vs. Hurricane

Tornadoes and hurricanes both produce violent winds, but they are radically different phenomena — different scales, different formation, different warning times, different damage patterns. Here's what actually distinguishes them.

Size

Hurricanes: 100 to 900 miles across. Hurricane Sandy (2012) was 1,000 miles wide at peak.

Tornadoes: Rarely more than 1 mile wide. The widest tornado ever measured (El Reno 2013) was 2.6 miles.

Duration

Hurricanes: Days to weeks. Individual storms can persist for 2+ weeks over the Atlantic before landfall.

Tornadoes: Minutes to a few hours. Most last less than 10 minutes. The longest-tracked tornado ever recorded was on the ground for about 3.5 hours (Tri-State 1925).

Wind Speed

Hurricanes: 74–200+ mph sustained winds. Peak measured: 216 mph (Hurricane Patricia 2015 at landfall).

Tornadoes: 65–300+ mph. Peak measured: 301 mph (Bridge Creek–Moore 1999).

Violent tornadoes have higher peak wind speeds than the strongest hurricanes — but over a much smaller area and for much less time.

Formation

Hurricanes: Form over warm tropical ocean water (typically ≥80°F sea surface temperature). Require 5+ days of favorable atmospheric conditions.

Tornadoes: Form from supercell thunderstorms over land. Require strong wind shear and atmospheric instability. Can form in minutes.

Warning Time

Hurricanes: Days to weeks of advance notice. Modern hurricane tracks are predictable within tens of miles by 5-day forecasts.

Tornadoes: 10–30 minutes average NWS lead time. Some events give less than 5 minutes' warning.

Damage Patterns

Hurricanes: Widespread damage across huge areas — storm surge flooding, sustained wind damage over hundreds of miles, extensive tree loss, prolonged power outages. Rebuilding takes years.

Tornadoes: Narrow, intense damage swaths. Total destruction along a path 100 yards to 2 miles wide, with untouched neighborhoods immediately adjacent.

Deadliness

Hurricanes: Hurricane Katrina (2005) killed 1,833. Hurricane Maria (2017) killed ~3,000 in Puerto Rico. Historic hurricanes killed tens of thousands (1900 Galveston: ~8,000).

Tornadoes: The deadliest US tornado (Tri-State 1925) killed 695. Deadliest single-day tornado death toll: April 27, 2011 (316).

Geographic Range

Hurricanes: Coastal areas of the Atlantic, Gulf, and Pacific — no direct impact on interior US states except from remnants.

Tornadoes: All 50 states have recorded tornadoes. Densest activity in the Central Plains (Tornado Alley) and the Southeast.

Rating Scales

Hurricanes: Saffir-Simpson Scale, Category 1 (74–95 mph) through Category 5 (>157 mph).

Tornadoes: Enhanced Fujita (EF) Scale, EF0 (65–85 mph) through EF5 (>200 mph). See our EF Scale explainer.

Can Hurricanes Cause Tornadoes?

Yes. Hurricanes routinely spawn tornadoes as they make landfall — the outer rain bands provide the wind shear needed for tornado formation. Hurricane Ivan (2004) alone spawned 118 tornadoes. Most hurricane-spawned tornadoes are EF0–EF2, but some are stronger.

Which Kills More People in the US?

Hurricanes. Individual hurricanes can kill hundreds to thousands. Tornadoes typically kill dozens per year in the US, though the deadliest years (2011) approach 500. Hurricane damage is also more expensive per event.

Which is more dangerous to you personally? Depends entirely on where you live. Coastal Florida faces higher hurricane risk. Oklahoma faces higher tornado risk. Alabama unfortunately faces both.
