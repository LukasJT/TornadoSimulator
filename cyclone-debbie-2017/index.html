Cyclone Debbie (2017)

Category 4 cyclone · Queensland, NSW, and New Zealand · March-April 2017 · 14 killed

Cyclone Debbie is unusual among Southern Hemisphere cyclones for the sheer geographic reach of its damage. It struck Queensland's Whitsundays as a Category 4 system on March 28, 2017, then tracked south causing severe flooding in New South Wales — and, days later, its remnants crossed the Tasman Sea and contributed to the flooding of a New Zealand town.

Landfall in the Whitsundays

Debbie crossed the Queensland coast near Airlie Beach and the Whitsunday Islands, a major tourism region, as a Category 4 cyclone. It moved slowly at landfall, which prolonged the destructive winds over affected communities and caused extensive damage to resorts, marinas, homes, and the region's tourism infrastructure. Hamilton Island recorded severe wind gusts, and the Whitsunday tourism sector faced a lengthy rebuild.

The Flooding Phase

The more lethal phase came after Debbie weakened. As an ex-cyclone, the system tracked south down the eastern seaboard, dumping enormous rainfall totals across southeast Queensland and northern New South Wales. Lismore and the Northern Rivers flooded significantly — five years before the same town's record-shattering 2022 flood — and further flooding affected areas around the Gold Coast and northern NSW.

  • 14 deaths across Australia and New Zealand attributed to Debbie and its remnants
  • Category 4 landfall causing severe damage across the Whitsundays
  • Major flooding in southeast Queensland and the NSW Northern Rivers
  • Remnants contributing to flooding in New Zealand's Bay of Plenty in early April

Reaching New Zealand

Debbie's most unusual feature was what happened next. Its remnants moved out across the Tasman Sea and reached New Zealand in early April 2017, delivering heavy rainfall to the North Island. In the Bay of Plenty town of Edgecumbe, a stopbank on the Rangitaiki River was breached, flooding the town and forcing a large-scale evacuation.

That a single named cyclone could cause a Category 4 landfall in Queensland, major river flooding in New South Wales, and a stopbank failure in New Zealand within roughly a fortnight makes Debbie a standard teaching example of how far a tropical system's impacts can extend well beyond, and long after, its tropical phase.

Why Ex-Cyclones Are Underestimated

Public attention tends to focus on the landfall category — the wind number. Debbie illustrates why that framing is incomplete: its Category 4 winds caused the most spectacular damage, but its rainfall, delivered days later and hundreds of kilometres away as a weakening system, drove much of the flooding and a substantial share of the deaths. The same pattern appears repeatedly, from Cyclone Bola in New Zealand to ex-tropical systems reaching the UK.
