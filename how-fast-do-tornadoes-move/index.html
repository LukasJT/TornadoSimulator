How Fast Do Tornadoes Move?

How fast tornadoes travel across the ground, why movement differs from wind speed, and why storm motion matters during a warning.

Quick answer
A tornado’s forward speed is commonly measured in tens of miles per hour, but some move slowly and others race much faster. That travel speed is separate from the much stronger rotating wind inside the tornado.

Two different speeds

Forward speed describes how the circulation moves across the landscape. Rotational wind speed describes air moving around the vortex. Confusing the two can make a moderate translation speed sound like weak winds, or vice versa.

The parent thunderstorm and surrounding winds largely control track direction and speed. Tornadoes often move generally with the storm, but tracks can curve or change.

Why estimates vary

Radar tracks rotation at intervals and spotters report changing positions. Survey teams later compare timestamps and damage locations. Every method has uncertainty, especially when a tornado is hidden or forms between radar scans.

The motion printed in a warning is an estimate for the storm circulation, not a guarantee that the tornado will follow a straight centerline.

Do not calculate an escape

A projected arrival time is useful for getting into shelter, not for trying to outrun a tornado. Roads may be blocked by traffic, hail, flooding, or debris, and a track can change.

If a warning includes you, shelter where you are in a sturdy building. Move mobile-home residents to planned sturdy shelter before the storm reaches the area.

If a warning includes you

  1. Move to the best available shelter immediately.
  2. Get low and protect your head and neck from debris.
  3. Keep monitoring official updates from shelter.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 30 mph tornado weak?

Forward motion does not rate intensity. A slowly translating tornado can be violent, and a fast one can be weak or strong.

Can tornadoes stop moving?

Some move very slowly or appear nearly stationary for a time, which can concentrate damage in one area.

Do tornadoes always move northeast?

No. Many U.S. storms move generally east or northeast, but tornadoes can travel in many directions.
