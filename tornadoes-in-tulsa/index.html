Tornadoes in Tulsa

A researched guide to the tornado history of Tulsa, Oklahoma, with reporting context and official records.

Boundary note: Census rank and population refer to Tulsa's incorporated or consolidated-government geography. Tornado context may include the surrounding county or metro and is labeled accordingly.

The Tulsa tornado record

Tulsa's tornado history should be read as a path-based record rather than a single citywide count. Storms can begin outside the city, cross a corner of its limits or affect a suburb that shares the city name in regional reporting. This guide keeps those distinctions visible.

The city lies in the central and southern Plains. Gulf moisture can meet a dryline, front and strong winds aloft, creating organized supercells. The usual window is April through June, with a smaller cool-season window, although tornadoes can occur outside the peak when the necessary ingredients overlap.

For a complete chronology, query the NOAA Storm Events Database by state, county and date. Follow a promising record to the responsible National Weather Service office’s survey, which is the best source for the path, rating, width and damage indicators.

From sparse reports to modern surveys

Before the modern warning era, a tornado might enter the record only through newspaper accounts, deaths or major structural damage. The growth of warning radar, spotter networks and formal surveys made the record more complete. The Fujita scale was introduced in the 1970s; the United States adopted the Enhanced Fujita scale in 2007.

Development also changes what a similar track encounters. With an estimated 416,209 residents inside the 2025 Census city geography, Tulsa ranks #48 nationally. That population figure describes exposure, not tornado probability.

How to verify a reported Tulsa tornado

  1. Record the event date and the county or counties crossed.
  2. Open the official Storm Events entry and confirm that the event type is tornado.
  3. Read the local damage survey and compare its path map with the incorporated boundary.
  4. Treat preliminary ratings and social-media locations as provisional until the survey is complete.
