The Winterset, Iowa Tornado of March 5, 2022

EF4 · Winterset, Iowa · March 5, 2022 · 6 fatalities, record outbreak day

On the afternoon of March 5, 2022, an EF4 tornado tracked through rural Madison County, Iowa, near the small city of Winterset — the birthplace of actor John Wayne — killing six people. The tornado was the most powerful and deadliest of an extraordinary, record-setting outbreak that produced more confirmed tornadoes across Iowa in a single day than any day in the state's recorded history.

What Happened

An unusually potent early-season storm system moved across Iowa on March 5, 2022, tapping into strong wind shear and enough instability to support numerous supercells across the state. Iowa's National Weather Service offices ultimately confirmed at least two dozen tornadoes from the event — an outbreak scale far more typical of the Southern Plains in May than Iowa in early March.

The deadliest of these tornadoes formed southwest of Des Moines and tracked through rural Madison County, striking several farms and rural homes directly. National Weather Service survey teams rated the tornado EF4, with winds estimated in the 166-200 mph range — among the strongest tornadoes to hit Iowa in recent memory.

Damage and Fatalities

Unlike an urban direct hit, the Winterset-area tornado tracked mostly through open farmland — but where it struck occupied rural homes, the destruction was near-total. Six people were killed, several rural residences were leveled to their foundations, and outbuildings, grain bins, and farm equipment across the tornado's path were destroyed or scattered.

  • Six fatalities in rural Madison County, southwest of Des Moines
  • Multiple rural homes destroyed, some leveled to their foundations
  • Widespread damage to farm outbuildings, grain storage, and equipment
  • Part of a record-setting, at-least-two-dozen-tornado outbreak day across Iowa

A Record-Breaking Outbreak Day

What set March 5, 2022 apart wasn't just the strength of the Madison County tornado — it was the sheer number of tornadoes confirmed across Iowa in a single calendar day, a total that exceeded anything previously on record for the state. The outbreak underscored that Iowa, while more associated with summer severe weather, is fully within reach of large-scale, high-end tornado outbreaks when the right ingredients align, even in early March.

Why Early-Season Tornadoes Catch People Off Guard

March tornadoes in the Upper Midwest sit outside the period when most residents are primed to think about tornado risk, which researchers and emergency managers point to as a factor in outbreak preparedness generally. Storm shelters, safe-room construction, and household tornado plans are all somewhat less top-of-mind in early spring than during the traditional peak season — even though, as March 5, 2022 demonstrated, the atmosphere does not always wait for the calendar.

Aftermath

Madison County and the state of Iowa received disaster assistance to support recovery in the rural communities hit hardest by the outbreak. Because the worst damage was spread across dispersed farms rather than a single town center, recovery looked different than in tornado disasters that flatten a downtown: individual families rebuilding isolated homes and farm operations, rather than a single, concentrated community rebuilding effort.
