Airport Ground Stop Explained: Weather Delays, Route Closures, and Air Traffic Flow

What an airport ground stop means, why weather can trigger one, and how it differs from a delay, ground delay program, or cancellation.

Quick answer: A ground stop temporarily keeps certain aircraft from departing because weather, congestion, or route limits make arrivals unsafe or impractical.

What it means

A ground stop can apply to one airport, a region, a route, or certain flights headed to a busy area.

It does not always mean the destination airport itself has the worst weather.

Weather causes

Thunderstorms, low ceilings, wind shifts, winter weather, and airspace constraints can reduce how many aircraft can arrive.

Controllers may slow or pause departures until the flow improves.

Passenger context

A ground stop can ripple through connecting flights because aircraft and crews are shared across the network.

Check airline updates and leave room for schedule changes when severe weather affects hubs.
