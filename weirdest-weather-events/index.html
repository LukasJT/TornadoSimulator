Weather curiosities

Weirdest weather events

Well-documented weather weirdness — animals falling from the sky, mystery cold pools, ball lightning, and the day it snowed in the Sahara. All verified, none embellished.

When animals fell from the sky

  • 1876 Kentucky "meat shower" — meat flakes fell over Bath County. Explanation: vulture regurgitation swept up by convection.
  • Yoro, Honduras — annual "Lluvia de Peces" (rain of fish). Documented for a century. Explanation: waterspouts scoop fish from Atlantic bays.
  • 2005 Odzaci, Serbia — thousands of frogs. Explanation: waterspout over a nearby pond.
  • 1841 Boston "shower of blood" — red rain. Explanation: red dust from Sahara.
  • 1877 Memphis snake rain — thousands of snakes fell across the city. Explanation: mysterious.

When the sky got weird

  • 1780 New England "Dark Day" — sky went dark at noon. Cause: massive Canadian wildfires.
  • 1883 Krakatoa red sunsets — worldwide sunsets glowed red for years due to volcanic aerosols.
  • 1908 Tunguska Event — meteor exploded above Siberia, flattened 830 sq mi of forest. Local sky was "on fire" for days.
  • 2016 Kentucky green sky — during a supercell, entire towns turned emerald. Explanation: hail scatter.

Ball lightning

Documented for centuries but only recently observed under lab conditions.

  • 1638 Widecombe-in-the-Moor, England — ball lightning killed 4 people in a church.
  • 1955 Salt Lake City — softball-sized ball rolled through a house.
  • 2012 Qinghai, China — first ball lightning captured on scientific spectrograph.

Weather in wars

  • 1281 Kamikaze typhoons — destroyed Mongol invasion fleets bound for Japan.
  • 1588 Spanish Armada — decimated by Atlantic storms.
  • 1812 Russia — winter destroyed Napoleon's army.
  • 1944 D-Day — Eisenhower risked landing during a break in a storm.
  • 1942 Ardennes fog — grounded Allied air support, enabled Battle of the Bulge.

The very cold and very hot

  • 1943 Spearfish, SD — 47°F rise in 2 minutes (Chinook).
  • 1972 Loma, Montana — 103°F rise in 24 hours.
  • Marble Bar, Australia — 160 consecutive days above 100°F (1923-24).
  • 2005 Sahara snow — snow in Algeria for the first time in 30+ years.
  • 2020 Antarctic 65°F reading — highest ever measured on the continent.

Storms that made history

  • 1900 Galveston Hurricane — deadliest natural disaster in US history at 8,000 dead.
  • 1931 Central China floods — deadliest weather disaster ever, 1-4 million dead.
  • 1970 Bhola Cyclone — 500,000 dead in East Pakistan.
  • 1959 Typhoon Vera — 5,000+ dead in Japan.

Weather that changed history

  • 1815 Tambora eruption — Year Without a Summer, global cooling.
  • Little Ice Age (~1300-1850) — cooler climate shifted European agriculture, contributed to famines.
  • 1789 French Revolution — three years of crop failures preceded revolution.
  • 2003 European heat wave — 70,000+ dead, prompted major EU climate policy shifts.
