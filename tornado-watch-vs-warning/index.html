Tornado Watch vs. Warning: The Difference in One Minute

Tornado watch versus tornado warning explained: what each alert means, what action to take, and how a tornado emergency differs.

Quick answer
A tornado watch means be ready; a tornado warning means take shelter now if you are in the warned area. A watch may cover several states and several hours. A warning uses a much smaller polygon around an immediate threat.

The practical difference

The Storm Prediction Center generally issues tornado watches when the atmosphere may produce tornadoes. Local National Weather Service offices issue warnings when radar or reports identify an immediate threat.

During a watch, review your shelter, charge devices, turn on alerts, and keep checking the forecast. During a warning, stop preparing and move to shelter.

Why both products exist

Forecasts can identify an environment favorable for tornadoes before anyone knows which storm will rotate. Watches communicate that broader potential. Warnings narrow the message to a storm and location that require action.

A storm can produce a warning without a prior watch, and a watch does not guarantee that every place inside it will receive a warning.

Read the map correctly

Watch boxes are broad outlines. Warning polygons follow the projected path of a storm and may cross portions of several counties. Zoom in to your location and do not assume the county border defines the threat.

Keep more than one warning source: Wireless Emergency Alerts, a weather radio, a trusted weather app, and local media can back one another up.

If a warning includes you

  1. Move to the best available shelter immediately.
  2. Get low and protect your head and neck from debris.
  3. Keep monitoring official updates from shelter.

Frequently asked questions

Can I leave during a tornado watch?

Normal activity may continue, but stay weather-aware and avoid plans that would leave you far from sturdy shelter.

What is a PDS watch?

A Particularly Dangerous Situation watch highlights an unusually high-end setup. It is still a watch: prepare and be ready to act on warnings.

What if the sirens sound during a watch?

Local siren policies vary. Check an official warning source and move indoors; never rely on sirens as your only alert.
