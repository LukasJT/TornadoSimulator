January 2027: Cold Snaps Inside a Warm-Leaning Winter

Why a warm seasonal temperature outlook can still allow January cold waves, ice storms, and local snow during winter 2026–27.

Published 2026-10-17; based on outlooks available October 17, 2026.

At a glance: A warmer-than-average winter tilt does not cancel January cold snaps. Seasonal means smooth together very different weeks and cannot time a cold outbreak.

Read the national 2026–27 winter outlook for the full regional context.

Averages hide volatility

NOAA favors above-average December–February temperature across much of the northern United States. That does not imply every January day will be mild. A few warm weeks can outweigh a shorter period of severe cold in the three-month mean, while an ice storm can occur near the freezing line even in a warm-leaning season.

CPC expected a strong El Niño to continue into early 2027 as of its October advisory. El Niño changes the probabilities of broad circulation patterns; it does not lock the Arctic Oscillation, individual troughs, or local surface temperatures into one phase for an entire month.

Where precipitation type becomes tricky

Along the central and eastern storm corridor, the difference between cold rain and freezing rain may be a shallow layer of subfreezing air at the surface. In the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast, storm path can determine whether the heaviest precipitation falls inland or near the coast. Those are event-scale questions that an October seasonal outlook cannot answer.

In the Great Lakes region, NOAA's drier seasonal tilt for some areas does not eliminate lake-effect snow. Lake temperature, wind direction, and arriving cold air are short-range ingredients, and a localized band can be significant despite an overall dry-leaning season.

What to check in January

Watch CPC's updated monthly outlook for a closer January temperature signal, then move to local NWS forecasts when a storm or cold front is within a week. For a potential icing event, focus on local alerts and the latest ice probability and amount forecasts.

If you need a firm travel or school decision, seasonal probabilities are the wrong tool. They offer context, while the hazard forecast should drive the decision.
