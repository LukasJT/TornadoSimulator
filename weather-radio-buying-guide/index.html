NOAA weather radio buying guide

Your phone will fail. Cell towers go down. Power fails. NOAA Weather Radio still works. Here is what to buy.

Why weather radio still matters

NWR broadcasts on 7 dedicated VHF frequencies from over 1,000 transmitters covering 95% of the US population. It doesn't rely on cell towers, internet, or power grid.

SAME (Specific Area Message Encoding) technology lets your radio filter alerts to just your county. No false alarms from three counties over.

A weather radio with SAME programming and battery backup should be in every bedroom in tornado-prone areas.

Features to look for

  • SAME code programming — filters by county.
  • Battery backup — 24+ hours minimum.
  • External antenna jack — matters in weak-signal areas.
  • USB charging port — dual purpose for phone.
  • Public alert certification — the CEA "PA" logo.
  • Alarm clock function — makes it useful daily.

Programming SAME codes

  1. Find your county's 6-digit SAME code at nws.noaa.gov/nwr/coverage/site.html.
  2. Add codes for neighboring counties if you commute or travel often.
  3. Set your radio to only alert for the codes you programmed.
  4. Test the radio monthly (Wed 11am local is the NWR weekly test).

Signal reception

  • NWR frequencies: 162.400, 162.425, 162.450, 162.475, 162.500, 162.525, 162.550 MHz.
  • A whip antenna extended vertically works best.
  • External antenna helps in rural / weak-signal areas.
  • Metal-framed houses attenuate signal — put the radio near a window.
  • If your area doesn't receive NWR, an internet-based scanner or the FEMA app is the backup.
