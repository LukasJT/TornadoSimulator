Storm Eunice (2022)

Named windstorm · United Kingdom · February 18, 2022 · 122 mph gust, an England record

On February 18, 2022, Storm Eunice tore across southern England and Wales with the most extreme winds the region had seen in decades, producing a gust of 122 mph at The Needles on the Isle of Wight — the highest wind speed ever officially recorded in England. The storm killed at least 4 people in the UK and triggered London's first-ever red weather warning.

What Happened

Eunice formed as a rapidly deepening Atlantic storm and tracked across southern England and Wales during the day on February 18, 2022, as part of a UK storm-naming system sequence that also included Storm Dudley just two days earlier. The Met Office issued a rare red weather warning — its highest alert level, reserved for genuinely dangerous conditions — covering London and much of southern England for the first time since the current storm-naming and warning system began.

The peak gust of 122 mph recorded at The Needles broke the all-time record for the highest wind gust recorded in England, in records dating back decades.

Damage and Impact

Eunice tore a large section of fabric roofing from London's O2 Arena, one of the storm's most widely shared images, and caused widespread structural damage, downed trees, and power outages across southern England and Wales. Rail and air travel were severely disrupted, with many train operators telling passengers not to travel and hundreds of flights cancelled.

  • At least 4 fatalities in the UK, with additional deaths across northwest Europe
  • A new all-time England wind gust record of 122 mph
  • The first-ever red weather warning issued for London
  • Widespread structural damage, including the O2 Arena's roof

A Modern Storm Warning Success Story

Despite its severity, Eunice's death toll was far lower than either the Great Storm of 1987 or the Burns' Day Storm of 1990 — a difference meteorologists frequently attribute to the maturity of the UK's modern storm-naming and warning system. The red warning, extensive media coverage, and widespread transport cancellations ahead of the storm meant far fewer people were caught outdoors or on the roads at the height of the winds than in earlier, less-warned storms.

Eunice in Context

Storm Eunice is now the reference point UK meteorologists use for the country's strongest measured wind gust, and its red warning is frequently cited as a model for how the modern warning system is designed to translate an extreme forecast into public action before a storm arrives, rather than after.

Aftermath

Repairs to the O2 Arena and other damaged structures across southern England took weeks, and insurers processed a large volume of storm-damage claims in the weeks that followed. Eunice remains the headline entry whenever UK wind records are discussed, and is often compared directly with the Great Storm of 1987 and the Burns' Day Storm of 1990 as the three defining UK windstorms of the past 40 years.
