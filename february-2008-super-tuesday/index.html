The Super Tuesday 2008 Tornado Outbreak

The Super Tuesday tornado outbreak of February 5-6, 2008 produced 87 tornadoes across the Southeast, killing 57 people. Unusually deadly February event.

The Event

The outbreak began on the evening of Tuesday, February 5, 2008 - primary election day. Warm Gulf air surged north as a strong cold front pushed east across the Mississippi Valley. Multiple tornadoes touched down through the night and into February 6.

Union University in Jackson, TN

One of the most photographed events of the outbreak: an EF4 tornado struck Union University in Jackson, TN, damaging over 60 dorms and campus buildings. Approximately 1,000 students were on campus. Miraculously no fatalities occurred at the university - all students survived due to advance warning and effective sheltering.

The university lost several buildings but was able to reopen within weeks. Union University became a case study in successful institutional tornado response.

Other Significant Events

Millsap-Atkins Community EF3, AR

Killed multiple residents in rural Arkansas.

Sumner County, TN EF4

Devastated communities in northern Tennessee. Multiple fatalities.

Multiple Long-Track Tornadoes

Several tornadoes traveled 30-40+ miles across the Southeast.

Why Deadly for February

February tornadoes are typically less frequent and less deadly. Why 2008 was different:

  • Very warm February day - dew points reached 65°F+ across the Southeast
  • Strong wind shear - jet stream still active
  • Nighttime timing - many tornadoes struck at night
  • Public unpreparedness - many communities weren't on high tornado alert in February
  • Mobile home concentration - Deep South housing patterns increased casualties

Warning Response

NWS warnings were effective, but limitations included:

  • Many people were sleeping or at election events
  • Nighttime response was slower than daytime
  • Some communities had inadequate outdoor sirens for extreme events

Post-Event Analysis

Super Tuesday 2008 contributed to:

  • Enhanced NWS overnight staffing during severe weather events
  • Improved cold-season tornado preparedness in the Southeast
  • State-level education programs about winter tornadoes
  • Investment in community storm shelters

The 2008 Tornado Year Overall

2008 was one of the deadliest US tornado years in the 21st century:

  • February 5-6 Super Tuesday outbreak - 57 killed
  • March-May Parkersburg EF5 events - 9 killed
  • Mother's Day May 10 outbreak - 5 killed in Oklahoma
  • Additional smaller events throughout the year
  • Total 2008 tornado deaths: 126

Legacy

The Super Tuesday outbreak reinforced:

  • February tornadoes are dangerous in Dixie Alley
  • Nighttime outbreaks require special preparedness
  • Election Day timing coincided with dangerous weather
  • College campuses need robust tornado protocols
  • Warm winter days can produce major outbreaks

Super Tuesday 2008 is often cited alongside Palm Sunday 1965, February 21, 1971 (Delta outbreak), and other off-season events showing that violent tornadoes can strike outside spring peak season.
