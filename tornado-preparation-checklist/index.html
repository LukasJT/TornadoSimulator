Tornado Preparation Checklist

A comprehensive tornado preparation checklist - what every family in tornado country needs before severe weather strikes. Use this as a family emergency plan template. Whether you're new to tornado country or have lived through outbreaks, this checklist ensures nothing critical is missed.

Family Communication Plan

  • Designate an out-of-state contact person
  • Have all family members memorize contact's phone number
  • Establish a family meeting place after separation
  • Register on Red Cross "Safe and Well" website
  • Have backup phone numbers written on paper
  • Discuss where children shelter at school
  • Know where family members shelter during work hours

Shelter Preparation

  • Identify primary shelter location (basement, interior room)
  • Identify secondary shelter if primary unavailable
  • Clear pathway to shelter of debris/clutter
  • Install storm shelter if in mobile home
  • Purchase storm shelter for permanent home if possible
  • Test shelter accessibility with all family members
  • Practice tornado drills 2x per year

NOAA Weather Radio

  • Purchase NOAA weather radio (Midland WR120 recommended)
  • Program for your county
  • Set to alert automatically for tornado warnings
  • Test monthly
  • Replace batteries annually
  • Backup radio in different rooms

Wireless Emergency Alerts

  • Enable WEA on all phones
  • Enable tornado warning alerts
  • Test settings after phone updates
  • Understand alert types (imminent threat, extreme threat)

Emergency Supply Kit

In Shelter (Ready to Grab)

  • Flashlight and batteries
  • NOAA weather radio
  • Whistle (to signal rescuers)
  • Solid shoes and gloves
  • Small first aid kit
  • Cell phone charger
  • Small blanket
  • Pet leash if applicable

Home Emergency Kit

  • 3-day water supply (1 gallon/person/day)
  • 3-day non-perishable food
  • First aid kit (comprehensive)
  • Prescription medications (7-day supply)
  • Battery-powered radio
  • Extra batteries
  • Flashlights
  • Cash ($200+ in small bills)
  • Basic tools (crowbar, wrench, screwdriver)
  • Duct tape
  • Plastic sheeting
  • Sanitation supplies (garbage bags, wipes)
  • Change of clothes and shoes
  • Copies of important documents
  • Copies of insurance papers
  • Emergency contact list

Vehicle Kit

  • Water and snacks
  • Blanket
  • First aid kit
  • Flashlight
  • Basic tools
  • Cell phone charger
  • NOAA radio

Insurance

  • Homeowners policy with tornado/wind coverage
  • Renters insurance if applicable
  • Flood insurance if in flood zone
  • Auto insurance with comprehensive coverage
  • Photograph home interior/exterior for records
  • Photograph valuable items with serial numbers
  • Save digital copies of insurance documents
  • Review policies annually

Home Preparation

  • Secure loose objects that could become projectiles
  • Anchor mobile homes with tie-downs
  • Reinforce garage doors
  • Trim tree branches near house
  • Remove dead trees
  • Repair loose roof shingles
  • Ensure smoke/CO detectors work
  • Have fire extinguisher accessible

Digital Preparation

  • Backup important photos to cloud
  • Backup important documents digitally
  • Have digital copies of ID cards
  • Contact list saved digitally
  • Insurance policy numbers digital
  • Medical records digitally accessible

Special Considerations

Elderly/Disabled Family Members

  • Ensure shelter accessible
  • Have medications ready to grab
  • Communication plan for hearing/vision impaired
  • Family member checks on elderly during warnings

Pets

  • Pet carrier by shelter
  • Pet food and water in kit
  • Vaccination records
  • Recent photo of pet
  • Pet ID tags current

Children

  • Teach children tornado warning signs
  • Practice drills with children
  • Discuss shelter location repeatedly
  • Explain in age-appropriate way
  • Have comfort items in shelter

Post-Tornado Preparation

Have Ready

  • Insurance policy contact info
  • Damage assessment checklist
  • Camera for damage photos
  • Emergency contractor list
  • Hotel/lodging alternative plans
  • Cash for immediate expenses

Know in Advance

  • Where to file insurance claims
  • How to reach FEMA (if disaster declared)
  • Red Cross local contact
  • Local emergency management

Community Preparation

  • Know your neighborhood alert system
  • Coordinate with neighbors (mutual aid)
  • Join community emergency response team (CERT)
  • Understand local shelter locations
  • Know evacuation routes

Monthly Maintenance

  • Test smoke/CO detectors
  • Check weather radio functionality
  • Verify flashlight batteries
  • Update water supply (rotate every 6 months)
  • Refresh food supplies
  • Test cell phone charger

Annual Maintenance

  • Review insurance policies
  • Update emergency contact list
  • Update photos of home/possessions
  • Practice family drills
  • Update medical information
  • Refresh emergency kit

The Bottom Line

Tornado preparation is not one-time - it's ongoing. This checklist ensures nothing critical is missed. Print it, use it, share it. Preparation saves lives.
