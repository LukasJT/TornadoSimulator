Ottawa-Gatineau tornadoes September 21, 2018

Six tornadoes — two of them EF3 — struck Canada's capital region on a September Friday in 2018. Dozens were injured and entire neighbourhoods leveled, yet nobody died. It became Canada's modern warning-system success story.

The overview

  • Date: September 21, 2018, late afternoon into evening.
  • Location: Ottawa, Ontario and Gatineau, Quebec — the National Capital Region.
  • Tornado count: 6 confirmed in the region (part of a larger outbreak).
  • Strongest: EF3 at Dunrobin-Gatineau, EF3 at Arlington Woods/Nepean.
  • Deaths: 0. Injuries: about 30.
  • Power: a key Hydro Ottawa station was destroyed — 250,000+ customers lost power for days.

The Dunrobin-Gatineau EF3

  • Formed west of Ottawa and devastated the rural community of Dunrobin.
  • Crossed the Ottawa River into Gatineau, Quebec — a two-province tornado.
  • Destroyed the Mont-Bleu neighbourhood apartment blocks in Gatineau.
  • Winds estimated near 265 km/h.
  • Homes in Dunrobin were swept to their foundations.

The Arlington Woods EF3

  • A second strong tornado hit Nepean's Arlington Woods and Craig Henry neighbourhoods near dusk.
  • Destroyed the Merivale electrical substation — the cause of the mass outage.
  • Hundreds of mature trees snapped in a dense residential area.
  • Struck with residents home on a Friday evening — warnings sent people to basements.

Why nobody died

  • Environment Canada issued tornado warnings pushed to every phone via Alert Ready.
  • The 2018 event was an early large-scale test of Canada's wireless alert system — it worked.
  • Basements are near-universal in Ottawa-area housing.
  • Community awareness after decades of education.
  • Contrast with Edmonton 1987 (27 dead, no phone alerts) shows the arc of progress.

The aftermath

  • Multi-year rebuild in Dunrobin and Mont-Bleu.
  • Hydro grid hardening investments followed.
  • The Northern Tornadoes Project (Western University) used the event to showcase modern damage surveying.
  • Canada now confirms far more tornadoes annually thanks to NTP satellite and drone surveys.
