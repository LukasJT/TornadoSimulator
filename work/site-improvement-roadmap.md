# TornadoSimulator.net Improvement Roadmap

This is the long-running punch list for turning TornadoSimulator.net into a deeper weather education, severe weather news, simulator, and games site. The items are grouped by the kind of improvement they would make: trust, search, reader value, monetization, performance, games, data, and editorial operations.

## Editorial Depth

- Expand every short article into a full guide with a lede, context, science explanation, safety or decision guidance, and official sources.
- Give every article at least one visual figure, diagram, map, table, photo, or data graphic.
- Add a "What changed recently" box on time-sensitive pages such as yearly tornado season summaries.
- Add "What this means for you" sections to translate science into action.
- Add "Common mistakes" sections to reduce myths and low-quality advice.
- Add "Key takeaways" summaries for readers who skim.
- Add "How experts read this" sections for radar, outlook, satellite, and model pages.
- Add "When to worry" sections for alert and safety pages.
- Add "When not to panic" sections for commonly misunderstood hazards.
- Add "What forecasters still cannot know" sections for uncertainty topics.
- Add glossary popovers for terms like CAPE, CIN, shear, helicity, storm surge, wet bulb, and dew point.
- Add article-specific FAQ sections using real search phrasing.
- Add source boxes that list NOAA, NWS, SPC, NHC, FEMA, Ready.gov, and peer-reviewed sources where appropriate.
- Add last-reviewed dates to evergreen science articles.
- Add author/editor blocks explaining the educational purpose of the site.
- Add correction notes when an article is updated after new information.
- Add by-topic editorial templates so tornado, hurricane, flood, winter, heat, and radar pages all feel consistent.
- Add "related simulator" callouts where the simulator can reinforce the article.
- Add "related game" callouts where quizzes or namer games fit the topic.
- Add deeper event timelines for famous tornadoes and hurricanes.
- Add damage analysis sections to historic tornado pages.
- Add meteorological setup sections to every famous event page.
- Add survivor-safety lesson sections to event pages without turning them into sensational stories.
- Add rebuilding and recovery sections for disaster aftermath pages.
- Add "how warning systems changed after this event" to major history pages.
- Add more plain-English comparisons such as tornado vs derecho, surge vs tide, watch vs warning, and funnel cloud vs tornado.
- Add "good source / bad source" media literacy sections for viral weather claims.
- Add beginner, intermediate, and advanced versions of core topics.
- Add classroom handouts for top student topics.
- Add printable family checklists on preparedness pages.
- Add state-by-state verified links to local emergency management agencies.
- Add city pages that explain local tornado risk without exaggeration.
- Add seasonal checklists for spring, summer, fall, winter, hurricane season, monsoon season, and wildfire smoke season.
- Add "what to do in the first 5 minutes" sections for urgent safety topics.
- Add "what to do before, during, and after" sections to all preparedness guides.
- Add "how to read official alerts" modules on watch/warning pages.
- Add "what a warning does not mean" modules to reduce overconfidence and false-alarm confusion.
- Add "data caveats" to records, rankings, and climatology pages.
- Add "why rankings differ" explanations for deadliest, costliest, largest, and strongest weather lists.
- Add regional context to every state tornado page.
- Add separate pages for Canadian provinces, European tornado regions, Bangladesh, Argentina, Australia, South Africa, Japan, and New Zealand.
- Add wildfire weather articles beyond smoke: red flag days, downslope winds, fuel moisture, and evacuation weather.
- Add drought science pages for soil moisture, flash drought, hydrologic drought, and agricultural drought.
- Add more tropical cyclone pages: eyewall replacement, rapid intensification, cone of uncertainty, storm surge watches, inland flooding, and post-tropical transitions.
- Add more winter pages: lake-effect bands, ice accretion, snow ratios, ground blizzards, freezing drizzle, and winter power outages.
- Add more flood pages: urban flooding, river gauges, recurrence intervals, flood insurance terms, and rainfall rates.
- Add more aviation weather pages: turbulence, low ceilings, crosswinds, icing, convective SIGMETs, and ground stops.
- Add more marine weather pages: small craft advisories, gale warnings, rip currents, sneaker waves, harbor surge, and lake-effect hazards.
- Add more climate-pattern pages: ENSO, MJO, Arctic Oscillation, NAO, PDO, blocking highs, heat domes, and jet-stream patterns.
- Add more weather instrument pages: thermometer siting, anemometer exposure, barometer trends, home station calibration, and rain gauge maintenance.
- Add more forecast literacy pages: ensemble spread, probability, model bias, forecast busts, and confidence.
- Add more radar pages: reflectivity, velocity, correlation coefficient, debris signatures, hail cores, and range folding.
- Add more satellite pages: visible, infrared, water vapor, mesoscale sectors, overshooting tops, and smoke plumes.
- Add more student pages: science fair projects, experiments, worksheets, vocabulary, and career paths.
- Add more home and property pages: roof damage, gutters, sump pumps, drainage, safe rooms, and generator safety.
- Add more business pages: continuity plans, employee alerts, outdoor work heat safety, construction wind rules, and event weather plans.
- Add more community pages: cooling centers, shelters, volunteer coordination, donations, and neighborhood preparedness.

- Add image sitemap entries once real image assets are used.
- Keep page titles under control: descriptive, unique, and not stuffed.
- Keep meta descriptions unique and action-oriented.
- Add internal links high in articles, not only at the bottom.
- Add "next read" modules based on topic clusters.
- Add related games and tools where they match search intent.
- Add HTML tables where users are likely comparing categories, scales, or warnings.
- Add state and city pages in consistent clusters rather than random isolated pages.
- Add regional pages targeting "tornado season in X", "does X get tornadoes", and "where to shelter in X" queries.
- Add evergreen explainers for every alert phrase people search after receiving a notification.
- Add long-tail safety pages for apartments, mobile homes, high-rise buildings, schools, airports, hotels, offices, and RV parks.
- Add pages for weather myths that answer one question directly and then deepen the answer.
- Add pages targeting "near me" intent carefully without pretending to provide live warnings.

- Add fresh seasonal updates before spring severe weather season and hurricane season.

- Add Open Graph and Twitter images for major hubs.

## Ad Revenue Without Bad UX

- Prioritize the proven 300x250 Adsterra placement within article content.
- Keep medium rectangles near natural reading breaks, not interrupting every paragraph.
- Use side-rail ads on wide desktop screens.
- Avoid invisible click zones, popunders, forced redirects, and scripts that capture normal page clicks.
- Cap auto-inserted article ads so pages do not feel spammy.
- Reserve enough space for ad slots to reduce layout shift.
- Lazy-load ad slots when they approach the viewport.
- Keep mobile ads below the lede and between sections rather than above all content.
- Use house ads when third-party demand fails.
- Add RPM reporting by placement type, article cluster, and device.
- Track scroll depth so ad placement can be tuned to actual reading behavior.
- Track article dwell time to understand which content earns attention.
- Compare article topics by RPM: safety, history, hurricane, insurance, home protection, and tools may perform differently.
- Add ad density rules for short pages, medium pages, and long pages.
- Create a per-page ad quality checklist before adding new formats.
- Remove any ad format that creates accidental clicks.
- Keep legal, privacy, and contact pages lightly monetized or unmonetized.
- Keep games playable without blocking controls.
- Add anchor links around ads so article flow remains clear.
- Monitor Core Web Vitals after ad script changes.
- Add fallback messaging only if an empty ad slot leaves awkward whitespace.
- Test desktop, tablet, and phone layouts for ad overlap.
- Do not use auto sound, forced full-screen, or deceptive buttons.
- Add tracking labels to ad containers so performance can be reviewed by placement.
