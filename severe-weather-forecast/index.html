How to read the categorical outlook

The Storm Prediction Center rates each day's severe-weather threat on a five-level scale, plus a general-thunderstorm category. Higher levels mean a greater coverage and intensity of severe storms — not a guarantee for any one spot.

LevelCategoryWhat it means
TSTMGeneral ThunderstormsNon-severe storms expected.
1 / MRGLMarginalIsolated severe storms, limited in coverage and intensity.
2 / SLGTSlightScattered severe storms possible.
3 / ENHEnhancedNumerous severe storms, some intense.
4 / MDTModerateWidespread severe weather likely, including strong tornadoes or major wind/hail.
5 / HIGHHighA severe-weather outbreak is expected — the rarest and most dangerous category.

The probability maps show the percent chance of each hazard near you. For tornadoes, 2% or 5% is a real threat and 10%+ is significant; for wind and hail the percentages run higher. A hatched overlay marks where significant severe weather — EF2+ tornadoes, 75+ mph wind, or 2"+ hail — is possible.

Other forecast sources

The SPC is the authoritative US source, but these are worth watching alongside it:

Nadocast → A machine-learning tornado probability forecast for the US, built by Jim Koutavas. A useful second opinion on tornado risk, updated a few times a day. Environment Canada TSO → Experimental thunderstorm-outlook products from Environment and Climate Change Canada — severe-weather guidance for Canadian provinces. SPC Outlook page → The full Storm Prediction Center outlook page, including the forecaster discussions that explain the reasoning behind each day's risk.
This is not an official warning source. Maps are embedded live from the NOAA Storm Prediction Center for information only and may lag the latest update. During active weather, always follow your local National Weather Service office, a NOAA Weather Radio, and Wireless Emergency Alerts. A tornado warning means take shelter now.
