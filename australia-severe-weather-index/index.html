Australia faces one of the broadest severe weather risk profiles of any developed country: tropical cyclones in the north, catastrophic bushfires in the southeast during extreme heat, some of the world's most damaging giant hailstorms in its major cities, and tornadoes — usually weak, occasionally destructive — across the eastern states. This index tracks Australia's most significant and record-breaking severe weather disasters, with full write-ups for the most notable ones.

Record-Breaking Events

Cyclone Tracy

· December 24-25, 1974

A compact but ferocious Category 4 cyclone destroyed more than 70% of Darwin's buildings and killed 71 people, triggering Australia's largest-ever evacuation. Read the full story →

The Sydney Hailstorm

· April 14, 1999

Grapefruit-sized hail battered eastern Sydney, causing over $1.7 billion AUD in insured losses — still Australia's costliest natural disaster. Read the full story →

Black Saturday

· February 7, 2009

Catastrophic bushfires during record heat killed 173 people across Victoria — the deadliest bushfire disaster in Australian history. Read the full story →

Cyclone Yasi

· February 3, 2011

A huge Category 5 cyclone hit north Queensland, devastating the region's banana and sugar cane industries, yet caused only 1 direct death thanks to mass evacuation. Read the full story →

Other Notable Events

  • Ash Wednesday bushfires (February 16, 1983) — fires across Victoria and South Australia killed 75 people, the benchmark disaster before Black Saturday. Read the full story →
  • Cyclone Larry (March 2006) — a Category 4 cyclone that devastated Innisfail, Queensland, and shaped the evacuation response later credited for Cyclone Yasi's low death toll.
  • 2010-11 Queensland floods — some of the most extensive flooding in Australian history, including the sudden Toowoomba/Lockyer Valley "inland tsunami" flash flood. Read the full story →
  • Newcastle-Sydney East Coast Low (June 2007) — a powerful coastal storm that beached the bulk carrier Pasha Bulker off Newcastle, an iconic image of the event. Read the full story →
  • Black Summer bushfires (2019-20) — an unusually early and prolonged national bushfire season that burned tens of millions of hectares across eastern Australia.
  • Thredbo landslide (July 30, 1997) — a rain-triggered landslide destroyed two ski lodges in the NSW alps, killing 18; one survivor was rescued alive after 65 hours. Read the full story →
  • Cyclone Vance (March 1999) — brought Australia's highest recorded wind gust at the time (267 km/h) to Learmonth, Western Australia.
  • Brisbane hailstorm (November 2014) — one of the costliest hailstorms in Australian history outside Sydney 1999, causing well over $1 billion AUD in damage.
  • Cyclone Debbie (March 2017) — a Category 4 cyclone that devastated the Whitsundays region of Queensland before its remnants caused catastrophic flooding further south in NSW.
  • Cyclone Marcia (February 2015) — rapidly intensified to Category 5 before striking central Queensland near Yeppoon, one of the fastest such intensifications on record for the region.

Key Facts

  • Cyclone Tracy remains the deadliest cyclone to hit an Australian city directly and fundamentally rewrote Australian cyclone-resistant building codes.
  • The 1999 Sydney hailstorm remains Australia's costliest single natural disaster in insured-loss terms, ahead of most cyclones and bushfires, because it struck a dense, high-value urban area directly.
  • Black Saturday's death toll directly led to the creation of the "Catastrophic" (Code Red) fire danger rating, the highest level in Australia's bushfire warning system.
  • Australia records tornadoes most years, mostly weak, but notable tornado outbreaks have struck areas including Bulahdelah, NSW and parts of southeast Queensland.
