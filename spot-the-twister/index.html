About the game

Tornadoes come in many shapes, and their form tells you a lot about how they behave. Spot the Twister shows you a tornado and asks you to identify its type from four choices. Get it right to build your streak across ten rounds, then share your score.

The tornado types

  • Rope — thin and snaking, often the birth or death stage of a tornado.
  • Cone — the classic funnel, wider at the cloud base than at the ground.
  • Wedge — appears at least as wide as it is tall; often the most violent.
  • Stovepipe — a tall column of nearly uniform width.
  • Multi-vortex — several small suction vortices circling one center.
  • Waterspout — a tornado over water.
  • Landspout — a weaker tornado not tied to a supercell's mesocyclone.
  • Elephant trunk — a tapering, curving funnel that narrows toward the ground.

Frequently asked questions

How do you tell tornado types apart?

Look at the funnel's width relative to its height and where it is widest. A wedge looks at least as wide as it is tall, a cone is wider at the cloud than the ground, a rope is thin and often contorted, and a waterspout sits over water.

Which tornado shape is the most dangerous?

There is no strict rule, but many of the most violent EF4–EF5 tornadoes appear as large wedges, and multi-vortex tornadoes can carve narrow streaks of extreme damage. Shape alone doesn't determine strength — always take any tornado seriously.
