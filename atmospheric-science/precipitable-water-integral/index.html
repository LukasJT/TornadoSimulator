How do you estimate precipitable water from a humidity profile?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Environmental science · algebra · November 21, 2026

Precipitable water is the water-vapor mass in a column per unit area, expressed as the liquid depth if all of it condensed. A pressure-layer calculation weights specific humidity by pressure thickness.

Worked example

Hydrostatic balance gives layer air mass per area Δp/g. Multiply by layer mean specific humidity q. At q = 0.010 kg/kg and Δp = 200 hPa, use 20,000 Pa to obtain 20.4 kg/m² of vapor. At liquid density 1,000 kg/m³, that mass gives a depth of 20.4 mm. This is the contribution from one layer.

Practice questions

  1. What depth comes from 100 hPa with the same q?
  2. Add another 100 hPa layer with mean q = 0.004 kg/kg.

Worked answers

Answer 1

About 10.2 mm.

Answer 2

That layer contributes about 4.1 mm, so the two-layer total is about 24.5 mm.
