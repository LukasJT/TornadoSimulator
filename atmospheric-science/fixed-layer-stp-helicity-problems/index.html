Fixed-layer STP practice with low-level storm-relative helicity

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Storm modeling · algebra and sensitivity · November 21, 2026

Hold the other fixed-layer STP factors at one and the index becomes the 0–1 km storm-relative helicity divided by 150 m²/s².

Start with What happens at the knot-to-metre-per-second STP shear cutoff?.

Worked example

For CAPE 1,500 J/kg, LCL 1,000 m AGL and shear 20 m/s, the non-helicity factors all equal one in the site equation. Compare 75 and 225 m²/s² of 0–1 km SRH. The indices are 0.5 and 1.5. The output is dimensionless and can exceed one.

Practice questions

  1. Find the result for SRH 75 m²/s².
  2. Find the result for SRH 225 m²/s².
  3. Double CAPE to 3,000 J/kg and keep SRH at 75 m²/s².
  4. Use SRH of 225 m²/s² but raise LCL to 1,500 m.
  5. A report gives only 0–3 km SRH. What input is missing?
  6. Why can STP equal 1.5?

Worked answers

Answer 1

75/150 = 0.5.

Answer 2

225/150 = 1.5.

Answer 3

The CAPE factor doubles to two and STP becomes 1.

Answer 4

The LCL factor becomes one-half and STP is 0.75.

Answer 5

The equation uses 0–1 km SRH. Recalculate it from the appropriate layer and storm motion.

Answer 6

It is a product of normalized environmental factors, rather than a calibrated probability bounded by one.
