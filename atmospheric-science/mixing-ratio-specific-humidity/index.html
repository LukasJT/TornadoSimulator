Mixing ratio vs. specific humidity: conversion exercises

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · algebra · November 21, 2026

Mixing ratio uses dry-air mass in the denominator. Specific humidity uses the combined dry-air and water-vapor mass.

Start with Why does relative humidity change when a parcel warms?.

Advertisement

Worked example

Let r be vapor mass divided by dry-air mass and q be vapor mass divided by total moist-air mass. Then q = r/(1+r) and r = q/(1−q). At r = 20 g/kg, use 0.020 kg/kg before adding one. The result is 19.61 g/kg of moist air.

Practice questions

  1. Convert r = 20 g/kg to q.
  2. Convert r = 10 g/kg to q.
  3. Convert q = 25 g/kg to r.
  4. Find r and q for 12 g vapor mixed with 588 g dry air.
  5. A student computes 20/(1+20) and reports 0.95 kg/kg. Why?
  6. For the same moist parcel, which ratio is larger when vapor is present?

Worked answers

Answer 1

0.020/1.020 ≈ 0.01961 kg/kg.

Answer 2

0.010/1.010 ≈ 9.90 g/kg.

Answer 3

0.025/0.975 ≈ 25.64 g/kg.

Answer 4

r ≈ 20.41 g/kg dry air and q = 20 g/kg moist air.

Answer 5

The input must be expressed in kg/kg before combining it with the dimensionless one.

Answer 6

Mixing ratio is larger because the same vapor mass is divided by the smaller dry-air mass.
