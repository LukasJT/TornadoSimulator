How fast can surface heating warm a well-mixed boundary layer?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Environmental science · budgets · November 21, 2026

A slab energy budget estimates how fast surface sensible heat would warm a well-mixed boundary layer of fixed depth.

Start with What does a surface Bowen ratio tell you about storm inflow?.

Advertisement

Worked example

Use ΔT = HΔt/(ρcph), where h is layer depth. For H = 120 W/m², Δt = 3,600 s, ρ = 1.2 kg/m³, cp = 1,000 J/(kg K) and h = 500 m, the supplied heat is 432,000 J/m² and the slab heat capacity is 600,000 J/(m² K). The slab warms by 0.72 K. The estimate omits entrainment, advection and radiative changes.

Practice questions

  1. Repeat for a 250 m layer.
  2. Can a growing mixed layer be represented by using the final depth throughout?

Worked answers

Answer 1

Halving the initial depth doubles warming to 1.44 K.

Answer 2

A changing depth can entrain air at a different temperature. A consistent budget must account for changing mass and the entrained air.
