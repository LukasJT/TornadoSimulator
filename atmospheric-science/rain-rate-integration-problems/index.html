How do you calculate radar-rain accumulation from uneven time steps?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Environmental science · algebra · November 21, 2026

Convert each radar rainfall rate to accumulated depth by multiplying by the duration it represents. Sum those depths across the event.

Start with How do you estimate precipitable water from a humidity profile?.

Worked example

Treat each supplied rate as the interval mean or assume it is constant through that interval. At 20 mm/h for 15 minutes the contribution is 5 mm. At 8 mm/h for 30 minutes it is 4 mm. The estimate totals 9 mm. Keep missing scans distinct from measured zero rainfall.

Practice questions

  1. Convert the first interval to depth.
  2. Convert the second interval to depth.
  3. Add a 10-minute interval at 12 mm/h.
  4. Find the duration-weighted mean rate over the original 45 minutes.
  5. A student averages the two rates and multiplies by 0.75 h. Explain the difference.
  6. How should you treat a missing radar interval?

Worked answers

Answer 1

20×0.25 = 5 mm.

Answer 2

8×0.50 = 4 mm.

Answer 3

The interval contributes 2 mm and the new total is 11 mm.

Answer 4

9/0.75 = 12 mm/h.

Answer 5

Their simple average gives equal weight to the two samples, but the lower-rate interval lasts twice as long.

Answer 6

Flag it as missing or use an explicitly documented estimate. A missing scan is not evidence of zero rain.
