How do you estimate moisture flux through a storm inflow boundary?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Environmental science · budgets · November 21, 2026

Estimate water-vapor transport through a storm inflow face by multiplying density, specific humidity, normal wind speed and face area.

Start with How does turbulent mixing dilute smoke in a deepening mixed layer?.

Worked example

With ρ = 1 kg/m³, q = 0.012 kg/kg, inward normal flow 10 m/s and area 1,000,000 m², the vapor enters at 120,000 kg/s. Choose inward as positive. For a spatially variable face, sum the transport across patches using local values.

Practice questions

  1. Find moisture mass per cubic metre in the example.
  2. Find vapor transport through the full face.
  3. What if the wind is 60° from the inward normal at the same speed?
  4. With the original wind and density, find transport across 250,000 m² at 8 g/kg.
  5. An analyst uses total wind speed when flow is tangent to the face. Correct the term.
  6. Does the result equal the rate of rain falling from the storm?

Worked answers

Answer 1

ρq = 0.012 kg/m³.

Answer 2

0.012×10×1,000,000 = 120,000 kg/s.

Answer 3

Normal speed is 5 m/s and transport is 60,000 kg/s.

Answer 4

1×0.008×10×250,000 = 20,000 kg/s.

Answer 5

Use the normal projection, which is zero for tangent flow.

Answer 6

No. Imported vapor can remain gaseous, be stored, or leave the selected volume before precipitating.
