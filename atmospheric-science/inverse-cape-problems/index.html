How much CAPE would an ideal parcel need for a target updraft?

Invert the ideal parcel speed equation to calculate how much CAPE corresponds to a target vertical speed, then practice with six problems.

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Storm modeling · algebra and sensitivity · November 21, 2026

In the lossless parcel estimate, CAPE equals one-half the square of the target vertical speed. This calculation gives the energy per unit mass required by the ideal model for a parcel starting from rest.

Prerequisite. Review ideal parcel speed.

Advertisement

Rearrange the energy equation

Let C be CAPE in J/kg and w be the target upward speed in m/s. The ideal work-energy relationship is w²/2 = C, with zero starting speed and no losses. For 60 m/s, square the speed to obtain 3,600 m²/s² and halve it to find 1,800 J/kg. Doubling w quadruples C. Review the forward calculation.

Practice questions

  1. How much CAPE gives an ideal 30 m/s?
    HintSquare 30 before dividing.
  2. Find the CAPE corresponding to 60 m/s.
    HintSquare 60.
  3. By how much does the required CAPE rise from 30 to 60 m/s?
    HintSubtract the two energies.
  4. Convert a 144 km/h target before using the equation.
    HintDivide kilometres per hour by 3.6.
  5. A student writes C = (w/2)². At 30 m/s, by what factor is the result off?
    HintCompare the denominator of the two squared expressions.
  6. Can an observed 60 m/s updraft establish that ambient CAPE was exactly 1,800 J/kg?
    HintList forces and losses omitted from the exercise.

Worked answers

Answer 1

30²/2 = 450 J/kg.

Answer 2

60²/2 = 1,800 J/kg.

Answer 3

1,800 − 450 = 1,350 J/kg.

Answer 4

144 km/h = 40 m/s. Then 40²/2 = 800 J/kg.

Answer 5

Their calculation gives 225 J/kg, half the correct 450 J/kg.

Answer 6
