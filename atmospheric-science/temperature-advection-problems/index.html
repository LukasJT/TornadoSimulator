How do you calculate horizontal temperature advection?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · dynamics · November 21, 2026

Horizontal temperature advection is the negative dot product of horizontal wind and the horizontal temperature gradient.

Start with What does the continuity equation predict above low-level convergence?.

Advertisement

Worked example

For an eastward 10 m/s wind and a gradient of −2 K per 100 km eastward, the advection is −10×(−0.00002) = +0.0002 K/s or +0.72 K/h. Warmer air from the west replaces cooler air. Vertical advection and other heating terms can also change local temperature.

Practice questions

  1. Convert −2 K per 100 km into K/m.
  2. Find the example’s tendency in K per hour.
  3. Reverse the wind and keep the gradient.
  4. Use wind (6,8) m/s and gradient (+1,−2) K per 100 km.
  5. A student uses a positive dot product without the leading minus. What error results?
  6. Can strong wind have zero horizontal temperature advection?

Worked answers

Answer 1

−2×10⁻⁵ K/m.

Answer 2

+2×10⁻⁴ K/s times 3,600 gives +0.72 K/h.

Answer 3

The advection becomes −0.72 K/h.

Answer 4

The dot product is −10 K m/s per 100 km. Negate and convert to get +0.36 K/h.

Answer 5

The sign reverses. Their answer would describe cooling instead of warming in the example.

Answer 6

Yes. Wind parallel to isotherms is perpendicular to the temperature gradient.
