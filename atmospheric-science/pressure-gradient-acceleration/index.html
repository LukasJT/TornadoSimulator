How do you calculate the horizontal pressure-gradient force per unit mass?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Atmospheric science · dynamics · November 21, 2026

Horizontal pressure-gradient acceleration points from higher toward lower pressure and is proportional to the pressure change per metre.

Start with Geostrophic wind problems from a height contour map.

Worked example

Use a = −∇p/ρ. A pressure decrease of 2 hPa over 100 km eastward gives ∂p/∂x = −200/100,000 = −0.002 Pa/m. At ρ = 1.2 kg/m³ the acceleration is +0.00167 m/s² eastward. This is one term in the horizontal momentum budget.

Practice questions

  1. What happens if the distance doubles at the same pressure difference?
  2. At the original spacing, pressure increases eastward by 2 hPa. Which way is the acceleration?

Worked answers

Answer 1

The gradient and acceleration halve.

Answer 2

It points westward with the same magnitude.
