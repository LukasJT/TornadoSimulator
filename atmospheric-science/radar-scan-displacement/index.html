How far will a moving supercell travel between two radar scans?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Physical science · vectors · November 21, 2026

Multiply storm velocity by time to estimate displacement between radar scans. Work component by component, then take the magnitude if you want distance for a straight, constant-speed track.

Start with How do you convert meteorological wind direction into u and v?.

Worked example

Use east and north as positive and convert the elapsed time into seconds. With (12,5) m/s for 10 minutes, multiply by 600 s to get (7,200,3,000) m, or (7.2,3.0) km. The magnitude is 7.8 km. This assumes the same storm feature is tracked on both scans and velocity stays constant.

Hint for the exercises

Convert scan time to seconds before multiplying by metres per second.

Practice questions

  1. What is the east displacement after 10 minutes?
  2. Find north displacement for this interval.
  3. Find displacement and distance if the scans are 5 minutes apart.
  4. A storm moves (4,−3) m/s for 15 minutes. Find the displacement and magnitude.
  5. A classmate multiplies 13 m/s by 10 and gives 130 m. Identify the missing conversion.
  6. Why may straight-line displacement be smaller than path distance?

Worked answers

Answer 1

12 × 600 = 7,200 m, or 7.2 km.

Answer 2

5 × 600 = 3,000 m, or 3.0 km.

Answer 3

The vector is (3.6,1.5) km. With speed 13 m/s, distance is 3.9 km.

Answer 4

The vector is (3.6,−2.7) km. The magnitude is 4.5 km.

Answer 5

Ten minutes equals 600 s. The displacement magnitude is 7,800 m.

Answer 6

If the storm changes direction, the path bends. Its full length can exceed the distance between endpoints.
