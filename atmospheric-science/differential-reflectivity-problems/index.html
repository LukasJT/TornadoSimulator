Differential reflectivity practice: what does positive ZDR mean?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Physical science · waves and logarithms · November 21, 2026

Differential reflectivity ZDR compares horizontal and vertical linear radar reflectivity. A horizontal-to-vertical ratio greater than one gives positive ZDR.

Start with Why does averaging radar dBZ directly change the answer?.

Worked example

Compute ZDR = 10 log₁₀(Zh/Zv). A ratio of two gives +3.01 dB. Convert between ratios and decibels using the appropriate logarithm. Orientation, shape, mixed scatterers and calibration can affect the observation.

Practice questions

  1. Compute ZDR for a ratio of two.
  2. Compute ZDR for equal reflectivities.
  3. Find the ratio for +6 dB.
  4. Swap horizontal and vertical reflectivities for the original ratio.
  5. Why is +2 dB an incorrect answer for a ratio of two?
  6. Can positive ZDR identify hail by itself?

Worked answers

Answer 1

10 log₁₀ 2 ≈ +3.01 dB.

Answer 2

10 log₁₀ 1 = 0 dB.

Answer 3

10^0.6 ≈ 3.98.

Answer 4

The ratio becomes 0.5 and ZDR becomes −3.01 dB.

Answer 5

The number two is a linear ratio. Apply the logarithmic conversion to get +3.01 dB.

Answer 6

No. Combine reflectivity, correlation coefficient, environmental context and other evidence.
