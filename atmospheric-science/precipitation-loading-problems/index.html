How does precipitation loading change updraft acceleration?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Environmental science · algebra · November 21, 2026

Suspended condensate adds weight to an ascending parcel. For small condensate loading, subtract g times the condensate mass ratio from thermal buoyancy.

Start with How much cooling can evaporating rain produce in a downdraft?.

Advertisement

Worked example

Use qₕ = 0.004 kg condensate per kilogram of air and g = 9.81 m/s². The downward loading contribution is 0.03924 m/s². The simplified acceleration is 0.12−0.03924 = 0.08076 m/s² upward before pressure gradients and other forces.

Practice questions

  1. Find the loading contribution for 4 g/kg.
  2. Find the resulting acceleration in the original example.
  3. Double the condensate at fixed thermal buoyancy.
  4. Find the loading that cancels the original thermal buoyancy.
  5. A student multiplies 9.81 by 4 directly. Correct the input.
  6. Can the parcel still rise after net acceleration turns negative?

Worked answers

Answer 1

9.81×0.004 = 0.03924 m/s² downward.

Answer 2

0.12−0.03924 = 0.08076 m/s² upward.

Answer 3

0.12−9.81×0.008 = 0.04152 m/s² upward.

Answer 4

0.12/9.81 ≈ 0.01223 kg/kg or 12.23 g/kg.

Answer 5

Convert 4 g/kg to 0.004 kg/kg. The loading term is smaller by a factor of 1,000.

Answer 6

Yes. Upward velocity can persist while the parcel slows.
