How do you calculate storm-relative helicity from a straight hodograph segment?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · advanced diagnostics · November 21, 2026

A straight hodograph segment contributes the signed cross product of its upper and lower storm-relative wind endpoints to the discrete storm-relative helicity sum.

Start with How much does storm motion change helicity on the same wind profile?.

Advertisement

Worked example

For storm-relative lower wind (u₁,v₁) and upper wind (u₂,v₂), use u₂v₁−u₁v₂ with height increasing. Subtract storm motion from both winds before multiplying. For (0,10) and (10,10) m/s with zero storm motion, the signed term is +100 m²/s². With multiple levels, sum the segment contributions.

Practice questions

  1. Find u₂v₁ in the example.
  2. Find the signed segment contribution.
  3. Keep the bottom fixed and change the top to (5,15) m/s.
  4. Use storm motion (0,5) m/s with the original winds.
  5. A student reverses top and bottom. What changes?
  6. What if the relative endpoints are collinear with the origin?

Worked answers

Answer 1

10×10 = 100 m²/s².

Answer 2

100−0×10 = +100 m²/s².

Answer 3

5×10−0×15 = +50 m²/s².

Answer 4

Relative endpoints are (0,5) and (10,5). The contribution is +50 m²/s².

Answer 5

The signed contribution flips to −100 m²/s² under the same convention.

Answer 6

The cross product is zero even when wind speed changes.
