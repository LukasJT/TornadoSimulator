How can a slow temperature sensor smooth a gust-front passage?

Tornado Hub · Lab · Environmental science · measurement · November 21, 2026

A first-order sensor approaches a sudden air temperature change exponentially. After one time constant it has completed about 63% of the full adjustment.

Start with How do you build a contingency table for a supercell forecast?.

Set up the experiment

Hold the sensor initially at 24°C and force a sudden step to 16°C. Its time constant is 30 s. Predict whether the reading reaches 16°C after 30 s, then compute T = 16+8 exp(−t/30). At 30 s it reads 18.94°C. This assumes constant airflow response and a perfect step change in the surrounding air.

Practice questions

  1. What is the model reading after two time constants?
  2. When does the reading first reach 20°C?
  3. Why could a real gust-front passage differ from the step experiment?

Worked answers

Answer 1

16+8e⁻² ≈ 17.08°C.

Answer 2

8e^(−t/30) = 4, so t = 30 ln 2 ≈ 20.8 s.

Answer 3

Air temperature can change gradually and the sensor’s response can vary with ventilation or housing.
