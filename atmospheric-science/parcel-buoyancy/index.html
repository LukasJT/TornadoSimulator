How do you calculate parcel buoyancy using virtual temperature?

Use the parcel and environment virtual temperatures to calculate buoyant acceleration with units and a sign convention.

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Atmospheric science · calculus · November 21, 2026

For a small parcel whose pressure matches its environment, upward buoyancy is approximately B = g(Tᵥ,p − Tᵥ,e)/Tᵥ,e. Use kelvins for the denominator. A warmer virtual temperature means the parcel is less dense at the same pressure.

Prerequisite. Review parcel stability and the sounding.

From density to virtual temperature

In the parcel approximation, the upward pressure force per unit mass is gρₑ/ρₚ and the downward gravitational acceleration is g. Their difference is B = g(ρₑ/ρₚ − 1). At equal pressure, the virtual-temperature gas law gives ρₑ/ρₚ = Tᵥ,p/Tᵥ,e. These expressions use an environmental hydrostatic pressure gradient and omit dynamic pressure perturbations and condensate loading.

Worked calculation

Take upward as positive. A +3 K contrast divided by 300 K gives +0.010. Multiply by 9.81 m/s² to obtain +0.0981 m/s². This is the local acceleration contribution at one level. Review how buoyancy relates to CAPE and CIN.

Practice questions

  1. Hold the environment at 300 K and lower the parcel virtual temperature to 298 K. Find B.
  2. What happens at equal virtual temperatures? Can the parcel still be moving up?

Worked answers

Answer 1

9.81 × (−2/300) ≈ −0.0654 m/s². Its thermal buoyancy points downward.

Answer 2

The local buoyancy term is zero. A parcel can have nonzero vertical speed from earlier acceleration or experience other forces.
