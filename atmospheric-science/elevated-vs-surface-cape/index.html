Why do surface-based and elevated CAPE disagree on the same sounding?

Tornado Hub · Lab · Atmospheric science · calculus · November 21, 2026

CAPE depends on which parcel you lift. A near-surface parcel can remain too cool to develop positive buoyancy while a parcel drawn above a shallow inversion finds a buoyant layer in the same environment.

Set up the experiment

Use the same synthetic environmental sounding for both parcel choices. The example supplies idealized buoyancy profiles in height coordinates so you can isolate the integration. Use a single parcel at each source level with no averaging. Before calculating, predict which parcel has the larger positive integral. The surface profile has no positive area. The elevated parcel has +0.10 m/s² from 1.0 to 2.5 km AGL, giving 0.10 × 1,500 = 150 J/kg. These piecewise values approximate a profile and do not resolve its transitions.

Practice questions

  1. Keep the elevated layer thickness and halve its positive buoyancy. Recompute CAPE.
  2. Keep buoyancy at +0.10 m/s² and raise the top from 2.5 to 3.0 km AGL. Find CAPE.
  3. How would reporting just “CAPE = 150 J/kg” obscure this experiment?

Worked answers

Answer 1

0.05 × 1,500 = 75 J/kg.

Answer 2

The thickness is now 2,000 m, so CAPE is 200 J/kg.

Answer 3

It hides that 150 J/kg is associated with the elevated parcel. A surface-based diagnostic gives zero in the same example.
