Does a midpoint time step improve a parcel trajectory?

Tornado Hub · Lab · Applied math · introductory numerics · November 21, 2026

A midpoint step samples acceleration halfway through the interval. For acceleration linear in time, that single sample gives the exact average over the step.

Set up the experiment

Hold initial speed at zero and use a(t) = 0.2t m/s² with t in seconds. Predict whether start-point Euler underestimates the gain. With a two-second interval, Euler samples zero and gives zero. Midpoint samples a(1) = 0.2 m/s² and gives 0.4 m/s. The integral from zero to two seconds is also 0.4 m/s. For acceleration that depends on speed, a midpoint method must estimate the midpoint state.

Practice questions

  1. Use a four-second step. Find the midpoint estimate.
  2. Use constant acceleration 0.2 m/s² for the original two seconds.
  3. Is midpoint always exact?

Worked answers

Answer 1

a(2) = 0.4 m/s², giving 1.6 m/s.

Answer 2

Both methods add 0.4 m/s.

Answer 3

No. It is exact here for linear-in-time prescribed acceleration. Curvature and state dependence can introduce error.
