How do you compute a storm-time anomaly from station climate normals?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Environmental science · measurement · November 21, 2026

A temperature anomaly is the observed value minus its climate normal for the same station and statistic. Compare daily maximum to daily maximum using a named baseline.

Start with How can a slow temperature sensor smooth a gust-front passage?.

Worked example

For an observed maximum of 4°C and a normal of 11°C, subtract to get −7°C as a difference. Multiply the difference by 9/5 to obtain −12.6°F. The additive 32°F offset cancels when subtracting two temperatures.

Practice questions

  1. Find the Celsius departure from the table.
  2. Convert that difference to Fahrenheit.
  3. A different day reaches 20°C with a normal of 15°C. Compute both departures.
  4. A report states a departure of −18°F. Convert it to Celsius.
  5. A student adds 32 when converting the original departure. Correct the equation.
  6. Can a daily observed maximum be compared with a climatological monthly mean of all readings?

Worked answers

Answer 1

4−11 = −7°C.

Answer 2

−7×9/5 = −12.6°F.

Answer 3

+5°C or +9°F.

Answer 4

−18×5/9 = −10°C.

Answer 5

The 32° offsets cancel when taking the difference. Use just the scale factor.

Answer 6

Those are different statistics. Use the appropriate daily maximum normal for this anomaly.
