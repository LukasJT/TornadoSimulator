How do you estimate visible-light transmission through a cloud?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Environmental science · measurement · November 21, 2026

The direct beam passing through a cloud is reduced by a factor e to the power of minus optical depth. This is direct transmission before adding light scattered into the sensor’s view.

Worked example

Use T = exp(−τ). At τ = 2, T ≈ 0.1353, so about 13.5% of the direct beam remains. Optical depth is dimensionless and additive for successive layers along the path. Total illumination can include diffuse scattered light.

Practice questions

  1. Find the fraction at τ = 1.
  2. Find percent transmission at τ = 2.
  3. Find transmission through layers of depth 0.5 and 1.5 along one beam.
  4. What optical depth gives about 50% direct transmission?
  5. A classmate gets 739% transmission for τ = 2. Explain the sign error.
  6. Can this direct fraction predict the full surface daylight?

Worked answers

Answer 1

e⁻¹ ≈ 0.368.

Answer 2

100e⁻² ≈ 13.5%.

Answer 3

Add depths to two and compute e⁻² ≈ 0.135.

Answer 4

−ln(0.5) ≈ 0.693.

Answer 5

Use exp(−2), not exp(+2). Attenuation gives a fraction between zero and one.

Answer 6

Diffuse scattering and other radiative effects also contribute to received daylight.
