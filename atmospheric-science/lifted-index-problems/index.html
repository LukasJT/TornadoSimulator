Lifted Index practice problems using 500 hPa temperatures

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · calculus · November 21, 2026

The Lifted Index subtracts the temperature of a lifted parcel at 500 hPa from the surrounding air temperature at 500 hPa. A negative value means that parcel is warmer at the specified pressure.

Start with CAPE integration practice with a three-level sounding.

Worked example

In LI = Tₑ − Tₚ, use both temperatures on the same scale and level. If the environment is −14°C and the lifted parcel is −10°C, LI = −14 − (−10) = −4 K. The numerical temperature difference is equal in degrees Celsius and kelvins. State how the parcel was chosen and lifted. Changing that choice can change LI, and a single level cannot capture the full buoyancy profile.

Hint for the exercises

Put both temperatures at the same pressure and in the same unit before subtracting.

Practice questions

  1. Use the first row and identify the warmer air.
  2. Calculate LI for −18°C environment and −22°C parcel.
  3. The environment is −16°C. Compare parcel A at −13°C with parcel B at −20°C.
  4. A kelvin sounding gives 257.15 K for the environment and 262.15 K for the parcel. What is LI? What if both temperatures are converted to Celsius first?
  5. A student subtracts a 600 hPa parcel temperature from a 500 hPa environmental temperature. Identify the error.
  6. Two cases have LI = −4 K. Can they have different CAPE?

Worked answers

Answer 1

LI = −4 K. The lifted parcel is 4 K warmer than its environment.

Answer 2

−18 − (−22) = +4 K. The parcel is colder.

Answer 3

A gives −3 K. B gives +4 K. Parcel choice flips the index sign.

Answer 4

LI is −5 K either way, since the 273.15 shift cancels in a difference.

Answer 5

Both values must be evaluated at 500 hPa. Lift or interpolate the parcel consistently before subtracting.

Answer 6

Yes. LI compares one pressure level. CAPE measures positive buoyancy integrated through a depth.
