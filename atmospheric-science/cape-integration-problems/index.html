CAPE integration practice with a three-level sounding

Calculate CAPE by integrating a piecewise-linear buoyancy profile. Work six problems with hints and step-by-step solutions.

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · calculus · November 21, 2026

Convective available potential energy (CAPE) is the integral of positive parcel buoyancy through its free-convection layer. For a three-level exercise, multiply each height interval by its mean buoyancy and add the areas. The result is measured in joules per kilogram.

Prerequisite. Review CAPE and CIN.

Integrate the buoyancy profile

Let B be upward buoyancy acceleration in m/s² and z be height in metres. Approximate the profile as linear between observations. The area of each trapezoid is Δz × (B₁ + B₂) / 2. Because m × m/s² equals m²/s², each area is energy per unit mass. If a layer crosses B = 0, find the crossing first and use the positive portion for this exercise. The negative area below the LFC relates to inhibition.

Practice questions

  1. Use the table to find CAPE.
    HintTreat the profile as two triangles.
  2. Find CAPE if peak buoyancy rises to 0.30 m/s² and the layer heights stay the same.
    HintScale the triangle areas by the peak change.
  3. Find CAPE for B = (0, 0.20, 0) m/s² at z = (0, 500, 2,000) m.
    HintUse two different layer thicknesses.
  4. In a synthetic ascent B rises linearly from −0.10 to +0.10 m/s² in 1,000 m. Find the positive contribution.
    HintFind B = 0 before integrating.
  5. A classmate integrates using heights 0, 1 and 2 km and reports 0.20 J/kg. Fix the result.
    HintWrite out the distance unit.
  6. Two profiles have 200 J/kg of CAPE. Must their peak buoyancy be equal?
    HintCompare the areas of triangles with different widths.

Worked answers

Answer 1

Each area is 1,000 × 0.20 / 2 = 100 J/kg. Total CAPE = 200 J/kg.

Answer 2

Each area becomes 150 J/kg, so total CAPE = 300 J/kg.

Answer 3

Areas are 500 × 0.10 = 50 and 1,500 × 0.10 = 150 J/kg. Total = 200 J/kg.

Answer 4

The zero crossing is at 500 m. Positive area = 500 × 0.10 / 2 = 25 J/kg.

Answer 5

The unconverted answer uses kilometres. Multiply by 1,000 m/km to obtain 200 J/kg.

Answer 6

A triangular profile with base 4 km and peak 0.10 m/s² also has 200 J/kg. Peak buoyancy can differ at equal integrated area.
