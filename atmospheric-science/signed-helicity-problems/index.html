How do you combine positive and negative helicity contributions?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · advanced diagnostics · November 21, 2026

Add positive and negative storm-relative helicity contributions separately, then sum those two signed totals for net helicity.

Start with How do layer bounds change 0–1 km and 0–3 km SRH?.

Advertisement

Worked example

For segments +90, −40 and +10 m²/s², the positive total is +100 and the negative total is −40. The net is +60 m²/s². The negative total remains negative. Separate values can reveal cancellation that the net alone obscures.

Practice questions

  1. Find the positive subtotal.
  2. Find the negative subtotal.
  3. Find the total for the original sample.
  4. Replace the middle segment with −120 m²/s².
  5. A worksheet takes absolute values before finding net. What does that produce here?
  6. Does zero net imply all segments contribute zero?

Worked answers

Answer 1

90+10 = +100 m²/s².

Answer 2

Only −40 m²/s² contributes.

Answer 3

100−40 = +60 m²/s².

Answer 4

Positive remains +100, negative is −120 and total is −20 m²/s².

Answer 5

It reports 140 m²/s² of absolute contributions. The signed net is 60 m²/s².

Answer 6

No. Equal and opposite contributions can cancel.

  2. Only −40 m²/s² contributes.
  3. 100−40 = +60 m²/s².
  4. Positive remains +100, negative is −120 and total is −20 m²/s².
