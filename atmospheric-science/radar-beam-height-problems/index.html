How high is the radar beam above a storm at 100 km?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Physical science · waves and logarithms · November 21, 2026

Estimate the height of a low-elevation radar beam using both its elevation angle and the curvature of an effective Earth. State the antenna datum and propagation assumption.

Start with How is supercell rotation estimated from an inbound–outbound velocity pair?.

Worked example

For range r, angle θ and effective Earth radius R, use h ≈ r sin θ+r²/(2R) with all distances in the same units. At 100 km, θ = 0.5° and R = 8,495 km, the two terms are 0.873 and 0.589 km. Beam-centre height is about 1.46 km above the antenna. This approximation assumes conventional refraction and can depart from reality in anomalous propagation.

Practice questions

  1. Calculate the elevation term.
  2. Calculate the curvature term.
  3. Repeat both terms for 50 km.
  4. For the original beam and a 0.20 km MSL antenna, find beam-centre altitude MSL.
  5. Why is multiplying 100 by sin(0.5) in radian mode wrong?
  6. If the beam center is 1.46 km above the antenna, does it sample only that height?

Worked answers

Answer 1

100 sin 0.5° ≈ 0.873 km.

Answer 2

100²/(2×8,495) ≈ 0.589 km.

Answer 3

0.436+0.147 ≈ 0.583 km.

Answer 4

1.46+0.20 ≈ 1.66 km MSL.

Answer 5

The angle supplied is in degrees. Convert 0.5° to 0.008727 rad.

Answer 6

No. Its finite width samples a volume and local beam height also depends on propagation.
