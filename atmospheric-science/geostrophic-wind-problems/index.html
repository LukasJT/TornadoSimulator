Geostrophic wind problems from a height contour map

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · dynamics · November 21, 2026

On a constant-pressure map, geostrophic wind blows parallel to height contours when Coriolis and the horizontal geopotential gradient are in balance.

Start with When can a storm-scale model neglect Earth’s rotation?.

Advertisement

Worked example

Use ug = −(g/f)∂Z/∂y and vg = (g/f)∂Z/∂x with x east and y north. For f = 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ and height rising 40 m north over 200 km, ug = −19.6 m/s and vg = 0 if there is no eastward gradient. This assumes negligible horizontal friction and acceleration.

Practice questions

  1. Compute the northward height gradient.
  2. Compute the resulting eastward wind component.
  3. Double contour spacing at fixed height difference.
  4. For heights rising 20 m east in 100 km with no northward gradient, find the wind.
  5. A student reports 19,620 m/s after inserting the spacing as 200. Correct it.
  6. Why is the equation unsuitable at f = 0?

Worked answers

Answer 1

40/200,000 = 0.0002 m/m.

Answer 2

−9.81×0.0002/0.0001 ≈ −19.6 m/s, directed west.

Answer 3

The result halves to −9.81 m/s.

Answer 4

ug = 0 and vg ≈ +19.6 m/s, directed north.

Answer 5

Convert 200 km to 200,000 m before dividing.

Answer 6

The formula is singular and Coriolis cannot balance a nonzero horizontal gradient there.
