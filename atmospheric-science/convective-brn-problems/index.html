How do you compute the bulk Richardson number for convection?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · advanced diagnostics · November 21, 2026

The bulk Richardson number for convection compares buoyancy energy with a bulk shear energy scale. An assigned shear velocity difference enters squared.

Start with How do you integrate updraft helicity on a model grid?.

Worked example

Use BRN = CAPE/[0.5(ΔV)²] with CAPE in J/kg and ΔV in m/s. For 1,200 J/kg and ΔV = 10 m/s, the denominator is 50 J/kg and the ratio is 24. Use the specified bulk wind definition. This is distinct from the gradient Richardson number based on local stratification and shear.

Practice questions

  1. Calculate the denominator in the example.
  2. Calculate BRN.
  3. Double the specified shear difference at fixed CAPE.
  4. Keep 10 m/s shear and halve CAPE.
  5. A student divides CAPE by 10 m/s and calls the result dimensionless. Correct them.
  6. What does zero specified shear do to the ratio?

Worked answers

Answer 1

0.5×10² = 50 J/kg.

Answer 2

1,200/50 = 24.

Answer 3

The denominator quadruples, giving BRN = 6.

Answer 4

600/50 = 12.

Answer 5

The denominator needs kinetic-energy-per-mass units. Square the speed and include the one-half factor.

Answer 6

The denominator vanishes. The stated formula has no finite result.
