How soon can a supercell interact with a simulation boundary?

Tornado Hub · Lab · Storm modeling · upper undergraduate · November 21, 2026

A distance divided by storm speed gives a practical estimate of when a simulated storm reaches a boundary. Faster signals can interact with that boundary earlier.

Start with Why do saved output times change estimated storm peak intensity?.

Set up the experiment

Place the storm 36 km west of the east edge and keep its eastward velocity at 12 m/s. Predict whether it reaches the boundary within an hour. The centre takes 36,000/12 = 3,000 s, or 50 minutes. The outer structure can reach it first. Gravity waves or advected errors can also travel differently from the storm.

Practice questions

  1. The centre’s eastward speed falls to 8 m/s. Find the travel time.
  2. The structure extends 6 km east of the centre. How soon can its leading edge arrive at the original speed?
  3. What experiment helps identify boundary effects?

Worked answers

Answer 1

36,000/8 = 4,500 s or 75 minutes.

Answer 2

30,000/12 = 2,500 s or about 41.7 minutes.

Answer 3

Increase the distance to the edge or vary the boundary treatment, then compare the same storm interval.
