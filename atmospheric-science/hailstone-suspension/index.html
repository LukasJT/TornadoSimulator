When can an updraft suspend a falling hailstone?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Environmental science · algebra · November 21, 2026

A hailstone is momentarily suspended relative to the ground when rising air exactly balances its fall speed relative to the air.

Start with How does precipitation loading change updraft acceleration?.

Worked example

Take upward velocity as positive. If the air rises at 22 m/s and the stone falls at 18 m/s relative to it, the net ground-relative speed is +4 m/s. This assumes the stone already falls at its terminal speed relative to the air under the local conditions.

Practice questions

  1. What if the updraft slows to 13 m/s?
  2. Can a single upward speed tell you how long a stone will remain aloft?

Worked answers

Answer 1

13−18 = −5 m/s, so the hailstone falls relative to the ground.

Answer 2

Its path can encounter changing updraft strength and its fall speed can change as it grows.
