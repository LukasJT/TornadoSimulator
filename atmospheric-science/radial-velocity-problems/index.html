Doppler radial velocity practice with a moving supercell

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Physical science · waves and logarithms · November 21, 2026

Doppler radial velocity is the component of target motion along the line from the radar to the target. Use outward as positive in this exercise.

Start with Velocity folding exercises: how do you unwrap a Doppler measurement?.

Worked example

To project speed V onto the outward beam, multiply by cos θ, where θ is the angle between the motion and that beam. At 20 m/s and 60°, the radial velocity is +10 m/s. Near horizontal, and neglecting vertical motion, the geometry can be approximated in plan view.

Practice questions

  1. Find the velocity for 20 m/s at 60°.
  2. Find it at 120°.
  3. At 30°, radial velocity is +12 m/s. Find speed under this geometry.
  4. An eastward 15 m/s wind is sampled from a radar directly east of the target. Find radial velocity.
  5. A student uses sine of the given angle. Why is cosine appropriate?
  6. Can zero radial velocity imply strong wind?

Worked answers

Answer 1

20 cos 60° = +10 m/s.

Answer 2

20 cos 120° = −10 m/s toward the radar.

Answer 3

12/cos 30° ≈ 13.86 m/s.

Answer 4

Outward points west, giving −15 m/s.

Answer 5

The angle is measured from the beam axis. The projected component is adjacent to that angle.

Answer 6

Yes. Motion perpendicular to the beam has zero projection.
