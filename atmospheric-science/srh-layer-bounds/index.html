How do layer bounds change 0–1 km and 0–3 km SRH?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Atmospheric science · advanced diagnostics · November 21, 2026

The depth of integration changes storm-relative helicity. Extending a layer can add positive or negative contributions from the additional height intervals.

Start with How do you calculate the supercell composite parameter with effective-layer inputs?.

Advertisement

Worked example

Use a common storm motion and heights above ground. For successive one-kilometre sections contributing +80, −20 and +35 m²/s², the 0–1 km integral is +80 and the 0–3 km integral is +95 m²/s². If a requested boundary falls between observed heights, interpolate the wind at that boundary before integrating.

Practice questions

  1. Compute 0–2 km SRH.
  2. Find 1–3 km SRH.

Worked answers

Answer 1

80−20 = +60 m²/s².

Answer 2

−20+35 = +15 m²/s².
