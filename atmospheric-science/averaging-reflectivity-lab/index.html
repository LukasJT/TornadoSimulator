Why does averaging radar dBZ directly change the answer?

Tornado Hub · Lab · Physical science · waves and logarithms · November 21, 2026

To average physical radar reflectivities, convert dBZ to linear Z, average the linear values, then convert back. Averaging dBZ directly gives the logarithm of a geometric mean instead.

Set up the experiment

Use two equally weighted artificial samples of 20 and 40 dBZ. Predict which average will be greater. Convert them to 100 and 10,000 mm⁶/m³. Linear averaging gives 5,050 mm⁶/m³ and conversion gives 37.03 dBZ. Direct averaging gives 30 dBZ. Choose a weighting and averaging method appropriate to the product.

Practice questions

  1. What happens if both samples are 40 dBZ?
  2. Give the 40 dBZ sample only 25% of the weight.
  3. Why does the linear mean lie close to 40 dBZ in the original exercise?

Worked answers

Answer 1

Both methods agree at 40 dBZ because the values are equal.

Answer 2

Linear mean = 0.75×100+0.25×10,000 = 2,575, or about 34.11 dBZ.

Answer 3

The high sample has one hundred times the linear reflectivity of the low sample, so it dominates the sum.

  1. Both methods agree at 40 dBZ because the values are equal.
  2. Linear mean = 0.75×100+0.25×10,000 = 2,575, or about 34.11 dBZ.
  3. The high sample has one hundred times the linear reflectivity of the low sample, so it dominates the sum.
