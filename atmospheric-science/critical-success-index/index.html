How do you calculate the critical success index for rare storms?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Applied math · probability and statistics · November 21, 2026

The critical success index measures how many forecast or observed storm events were matched hits. A long run of correctly forecast quiet periods leaves the score unchanged.

Worked example

Let H = hits, M = misses and FA = false alarms. Every case in which a storm was forecast or observed belongs to exactly one of these cells. Their union has H + M + FA cases, so CSI = H/(H+M+FA). Using synthetic counts H = 6, M = 2 and FA = 4 gives 6/12 = 0.50. If neither forecasts nor observations contain events, the denominator vanishes.

Practice questions

  1. Replace two of the misses with hits and keep false alarms fixed.
  2. Add 100 quiet windows with correct negatives. How does CSI change?

Worked answers

Answer 1

There are 8 hits and no misses. CSI = 8/(8+0+4) = 2/3.

Answer 2

It stays at 0.50 because none of those windows join the event union.
