How does a tilted updraft turn horizontal vorticity upward?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Atmospheric science · vector calculus · November 21, 2026

Tilting changes vertical vorticity when horizontal vorticity crosses a horizontal gradient of updraft speed. The sign depends on their alignment.

Worked example

The tilting contribution is ωx ∂w/∂x + ωy ∂w/∂y. In east–north coordinates, an eastward ωx = 0.015 s⁻¹ aligned with an eastward w gradient of 0.004 s⁻¹ gives +6.0×10⁻⁵ s⁻². Across the other flank the gradient can reverse, generating an opposite sign. This isolates the tilting term from advection and stretching.

Practice questions

  1. Reverse the eastward gradient. What is the new contribution?
  2. Rotate the gradient due north while retaining eastward vorticity.

Worked answers

Answer 1

−6.0×10⁻⁵ s⁻².

Answer 2

The vectors are perpendicular, so their dot product is zero.

  1. −6.0×10⁻⁵ s⁻².
  2. The vectors are perpendicular, so their dot product is zero.
