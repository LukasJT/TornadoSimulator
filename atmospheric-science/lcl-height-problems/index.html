Lifting condensation level height exercises with dewpoint spread

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · calculus · November 21, 2026

For a near-surface parcel, estimate the lifted condensation level height above its starting level by multiplying the air temperature minus dewpoint by about 125 m per degree Celsius.

Start with Why do surface-based and elevated CAPE disagree on the same sounding?.

Worked example

As an unsaturated parcel rises, its temperature falls faster than its dewpoint. The temperature–dewpoint gap closes. A rough closing rate of 8°C per km gives the 125 m/°C shortcut. With spreads of 4°C and 10°C, the estimated heights are 500 m and 1,250 m. The shortcut estimates the level at which the parcel reaches saturation under dry ascent. Observed cloud base can vary with parcel origin, mixing and local humidity.

Hint for the exercises

Find the temperature–dewpoint difference first. A Fahrenheit difference converts by multiplying by 5/9.

Practice questions

  1. Estimate the LCL height for 20°C air with a dewpoint of 16°C.
  2. Keep air temperature at 20°C and lower the dewpoint to 10°C.
  3. An airport is 300 m MSL. A parcel starts there at 18°C with dewpoint 12°C. Estimate the LCL in both AGL and MSL.
  4. Use 68°F air and a 59°F dewpoint. Convert the difference before applying the shortcut.
  5. A spreadsheet gives a negative height when Td = 22°C and T = 20°C. What should you check?
  6. Two parcels have different temperatures but the same temperature–dewpoint spread. What does the shortcut predict?

Worked answers

Answer 1

The spread is 4°C. Multiply by 125 m/°C to get about 500 m.

Answer 2

The spread is 10°C, yielding about 1,250 m.

Answer 3

The parcel rises about 750 m. That is about 750 m AGL and 1,050 m MSL.

Answer 4

A 9°F difference is 5°C. The estimate is 625 m above the start.

Answer 5

The air and dewpoint values do not describe a normal unsaturated parcel. Check units, instrument quality and whether the observations are paired before using the shortcut.

Answer 6

It gives the same approximate rise for both. A more complete thermodynamic calculation can distinguish them using their temperatures and pressures.
