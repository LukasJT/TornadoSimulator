How do you calculate storm-relative inflow from ground-relative wind?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Physical science · vectors · November 21, 2026

Subtract the storm-motion vector from the ground-relative wind to see how air flows in the storm’s moving frame. Express both vectors in the same east–north coordinate system and units.

Start with How do you convert meteorological wind direction into u and v?.

Worked example

If ground-relative wind is (8,12) and the storm moves at (14,4) m/s, the air relative to the storm moves at (−6,8) m/s. Its magnitude is √(36+64) = 10 m/s. The vector points toward the northwest, meaning air following that vector approaches from the southeast side of the moving storm.

Hint for the exercises

Translate the vector origin by the storm-motion vector.

Practice questions

  1. Find the relative east component in the worked example.
  2. Find the relative speed and the quadrant the air moves toward.
  3. If the storm accelerates eastward to (18,4) while the wind stays (8,12), what is the relative wind?
  4. What ground-relative wind yields relative flow (−2,5) when storm motion is (12,1) m/s?
  5. A student adds storm motion to ground-relative wind. Correct the reference-frame change.
  6. What if both vectors are equal at one level?

Worked answers

Answer 1

8−14 = −6 m/s.

Answer 2

√(6²+8²) = 10 m/s, toward the northwest.

Answer 3

The relative vector becomes (−10,8) m/s with speed √164 ≈ 12.81 m/s.

Answer 4

Add storm motion to relative flow. Ground-relative wind is (10,6) m/s.

Answer 5

Subtract frame velocity from the ground-relative vector. Adding would produce (22,16) instead of (−6,8) m/s in the worked example.

Answer 6

The flow relative to the storm is zero at that level in this idealized calculation.
