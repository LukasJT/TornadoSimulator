Potential temperature practice problems with dry parcels

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · algebra · November 21, 2026

Potential temperature is the temperature a parcel would attain if brought dry adiabatically to a reference pressure, usually 1,000 hPa.

Start with How much does water vapor change a parcel’s virtual temperature?.

Advertisement

Worked example

For dry air, θ = T(p₀/p)^κ where κ ≈ 0.286. At 800 hPa and 280 K, compression to 1,000 hPa gives θ ≈ 298.5 K. Use absolute temperatures and consistent pressure units.

Practice questions

  1. Find θ for the parcel at 800 hPa.
  2. Find θ when the parcel is already at reference pressure.
  3. Find θ at 900 hPa and 290 K.
  4. A parcel has θ = 300 K at 800 hPa. Find its actual temperature.
  5. Why does using Celsius in the power-law calculation fail?
  6. What stays constant for an unsaturated parcel lifted reversibly without heat exchange?

Worked answers

Answer 1

280×(1000/800)^0.286 ≈ 298.5 K.

Answer 2

The pressure ratio is one and θ = T.

Answer 3

290×(1000/900)^0.286 ≈ 298.9 K.

Answer 4

300×(800/1000)^0.286 ≈ 281.5 K.

Answer 5

The scaling law operates on absolute temperature. Convert to kelvins first.

Answer 6

Its potential temperature stays constant under the dry adiabatic assumptions.
