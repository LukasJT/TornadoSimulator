Hydrostatic pressure practice problems for a storm sounding

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · algebra · November 21, 2026

In hydrostatic balance, pressure drops with height because each level supports the air above it.

Start with How do you find layer thickness with the hypsometric equation?.

Advertisement

Worked example

The vertical pressure gradient is dp/dz = −ρg. For a shallow layer of approximately constant density, multiply −ρg by the rise. At 1.1 kg/m³ over 500 m, the pressure change is −5,395.5 Pa, or −53.955 hPa. Keep Δp signed as final minus initial pressure.

Practice questions

  1. Find the pressure change over 500 m using 1.1 kg/m³.
  2. Find the change for half that depth.
  3. Start at 980 hPa and rise 500 m through the original layer.
  4. Descend 300 m through air at 1.2 kg/m³.
  5. A spreadsheet reports −539.55 hPa for the 500 m ascent. Fix the conversion.
  6. Why use smaller steps for a deep sounding?

Worked answers

Answer 1

−1.1×9.81×500 = −5,395.5 Pa.

Answer 2

−2,697.75 Pa or approximately −27.0 hPa.

Answer 3

980−53.955 ≈ 926.0 hPa.

Answer 4

−1.2×9.81×(−300) = +3,531.6 Pa.

Answer 5

Divide pascals by 100. The result is −53.955 hPa.

Answer 6

Density varies with height. Smaller intervals let each contribution use a more representative density.
