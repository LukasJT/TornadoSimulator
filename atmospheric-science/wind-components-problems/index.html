How do you convert meteorological wind direction into u and v?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Physical science · vectors · November 21, 2026

For wind speed S and meteorological direction θ, use u = −S sin θ and v = −S cos θ. Direction is measured clockwise from north and describes where the wind comes from. Positive u points east and positive v points north.

Worked example

Draw a compass with north up and east to the right. For a 10 m/s wind from 270°, the arrow travels from west toward east. Since sin 270° = −1 and cos 270° = 0, u = +10 m/s and v = 0. For a southeast wind, both component signs change relative to a northwest wind.

Hint for the exercises

Draw where the air comes from, then point the velocity arrow away from that direction.

Practice questions

  1. Find u and v for the 270° wind.
  2. Find u and v for an 8 m/s northerly wind.
  3. Find the components of a 10 m/s wind from 135°.
  4. What meteorological direction corresponds to (6,8) m/s? Round to the nearest degree.
  5. A calculator gives a nonzero northward component for an exactly westerly wind. What setting should you check?
  6. Can you infer the direction from a vector with u = v = 0?

Worked answers

Answer 1

u = −10(−1) = +10 m/s. v = 0.

Answer 2

u = 0 and v = −8 m/s.

Answer 3

u = −10 sin 135° ≈ −7.07 m/s. v = −10 cos 135° ≈ +7.07 m/s.

Answer 4

The flow goes northeast with bearing arctan(6/8) ≈ 37°. Its source direction is about 217° and its speed is 10 m/s.

Answer 5

Check the degree versus radian setting first. Small floating-point residuals close to zero are also possible.

Answer 6

No. This is calm wind and the vector has no direction.
