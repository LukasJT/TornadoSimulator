Linear interpolation exercises for wind on model levels

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Applied math · introductory numerics · November 21, 2026

Linear interpolation estimates wind between model levels by weighting the measured change according to the fraction of the height interval.

Start with How do you measure numerical convergence in a storm simulation?.

Advertisement

Worked example

At 400 m let u = 6 m/s and at 1,000 m let u = 18 m/s. At 550 m the fractional distance from the lower level is 150/600 = 0.25. Then u = 6+0.25(18−6) = 9 m/s. For vectors repeat the interpolation separately for each component.

Practice questions

  1. Find the fractional weight at 550 m.
  2. Compute u at 550 m.
  3. Find u at 700 m.
  4. If the upper wind is 2 m/s instead, find u at 550 m.
  5. A student uses 550/1,000 as the weight. Correct the reference.
  6. Is applying this equation at 1,200 m interpolation?

Worked answers

Answer 1

(550−400)/(1,000−400) = 0.25.

Answer 2

6+0.25×12 = 9 m/s.

Answer 3

The weight is 0.5 and the resulting wind is 12 m/s.

Answer 4

6+0.25(2−6) = 5 m/s.

Answer 5

Measure from the lower level and divide by the height between the bounding levels.

Answer 6

That is extrapolation. No upper observation brackets the target.
