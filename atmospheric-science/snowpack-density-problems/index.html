How do you convert snow water equivalent to snowpack density?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Environmental science · algebra · November 21, 2026

Divide snow water equivalent by snow depth and multiply by liquid water density to find mean snowpack density.

Start with How do you estimate precipitable water from a humidity profile?.

Advertisement

Worked example

For 0.045 m liquid equivalent and 0.30 m total snow depth, the equal-area mass balance gives ρsnow = 1000×0.045/0.30 = 150 kg/m³. Use depths with the same length unit and ensure both measurements describe the same pack and area.

Practice questions

  1. Find the ratio of water equivalent to snow depth in the example.
  2. Convert that ratio to bulk density.
  3. Find density for 40 cm of snow containing 80 mm of water equivalent.
  4. The original pack settles to 0.225 m without losing water. Find the new density.
  5. A student compares 45 mm with 0.30 m without converting. Fix their setup.
  6. Does the bulk density tell you the density of every layer?

Worked answers

Answer 1

0.045/0.30 = 0.15.

Answer 2

Multiply by 1,000 kg/m³ to get 150 kg/m³.

Answer 3

Convert to 400 and 80 mm. Density is 200 kg/m³.

Answer 4

1000×0.045/0.225 = 200 kg/m³.

Answer 5

Use 45/300 or 0.045/0.30. Both ratios give 0.15.

Answer 6

No. It is total mass divided by total volume. Individual layers can differ.
