Vertical vorticity from a wind grid: finite-difference exercises

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · vector calculus · November 21, 2026

Estimate vertical vorticity on a wind grid by subtracting the northward derivative of eastward wind from the eastward derivative of northward wind.

Start with How does a tilted updraft turn horizontal vorticity upward?.

Advertisement

Worked example

Use ζ = ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y with x east and y north. On a centered stencil, each derivative is the difference between opposing edge samples divided by their full separation. For ∂v/∂x = 0.004 and ∂u/∂y = −0.002 s⁻¹, the result is +0.006 s⁻¹. Positive is counterclockwise viewed from above.

Practice questions

  1. Compute ζ using both gradients in the table.
  2. Find ζ if both derivatives are +0.004 s⁻¹.
  3. Use v east = 4 and v west = −2 m/s, u north = 3 and u south = 1 m/s with 1 km full separations.
  4. Use ∂v/∂x = −0.003 and ∂u/∂y = 0.002 s⁻¹. Identify the rotation sense.
  5. A student divides the table differences by 1 km. What went wrong?
  6. Can zero vertical vorticity rule out horizontal vorticity?

Worked answers

Answer 1

0.004−(−0.002) = 0.006 s⁻¹.

Answer 2

They cancel, giving zero.

Answer 3

The derivatives are 0.006 and 0.002 s⁻¹. Their difference is 0.004 s⁻¹.

Answer 4

−0.005 s⁻¹, clockwise when viewed from above.

Answer 5

The neighbors are each 1 km from the target in opposite directions. The difference spans 2 km.

Answer 6

No. Vertical gradients of horizontal flow can produce horizontal vorticity even where the horizontal derivatives cancel.
