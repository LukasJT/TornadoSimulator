How does horizontal wind shear create horizontal vorticity?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Atmospheric science · vector calculus · November 21, 2026

A wind that varies with height can have horizontal vorticity even in a horizontally uniform environment. For eastward u and northward v, the horizontal components of curl in that case are (−dv/dz, du/dz).

Worked example

With u increasing by 10 m/s per kilometre and v constant, du/dz = 0.01 s⁻¹ and dv/dz = 0. The vorticity vector points north. The full three-dimensional horizontal curl also contains horizontal gradients of vertical velocity w. Those gradients vanish in this exercise.

Practice questions

  1. What if v increases by 6 m/s per km and u is constant?
  2. What if a uniform eastward wind is added at every height?

Worked answers

Answer 1

The vector is (−0.006,0) s⁻¹, pointing west.

Answer 2

The derivatives are unchanged, so its vorticity is unchanged.
