What does the continuity equation predict above low-level convergence?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Atmospheric science · dynamics · November 21, 2026

In incompressible flow, horizontal convergence makes vertical velocity increase with height. Integrate the convergence and apply a specified lower boundary velocity.

Start with How do you estimate a gust front’s density-current speed?.

Advertisement

Worked example

Continuity gives ∂w/∂z = −(∂u/∂x+∂v/∂y). For divergence −0.002 s⁻¹ across 500 m and w = 0 at the base, w rises to +1 m/s. This is a constant-density estimate. A variable-density treatment uses mass fluxes.

Practice questions

  1. Find the change for convergence of 0.001 s⁻¹ through 200 m.
  2. If w is −0.4 m/s at the base of the original layer, find w at the top.

Worked answers

Answer 1

Vertical velocity increases by 0.20 m/s.

Answer 2

−0.4+1.0 = +0.6 m/s.

  1. Vertical velocity increases by 0.20 m/s.
  2. −0.4+1.0 = +0.6 m/s.
