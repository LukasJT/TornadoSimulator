How much cooling can evaporating rain produce in a downdraft?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Environmental science · algebra · November 21, 2026

Evaporating rain takes energy from the surrounding air and can cool a downdraft. Approximate the cooling by dividing the latent energy used by the air’s specific heat.

Start with How do you calculate radar-rain accumulation from uneven time steps?.

Worked example

If 1 g/kg evaporates, the latent energy use is 0.001×2.5×10⁶ = 2,500 J/kg of air. Divide by cp = 1004 J/(kg K) to estimate a temperature decrease of 2.49 K. This closed energy estimate holds pressure approximately fixed and omits mixing and other phase changes.

Practice questions

  1. What is the cooling for 0.5 g/kg with the same constants?
  2. Why does the estimate weaken when little rain can evaporate into nearly saturated air?

Worked answers

Answer 1

Approximately −1.25 K.

Answer 2

Less water evaporates, reducing the latent heat taken from the air.

  1. Approximately −1.25 K.
  2. Less water evaporates, reducing the latent heat taken from the air.
