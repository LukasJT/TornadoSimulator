How much does storm motion change helicity on the same wind profile?

Tornado Hub · Lab · Atmospheric science · advanced diagnostics · November 21, 2026

Changing storm motion changes the origin from which a hodograph’s signed area is measured. The underlying wind profile can stay fixed while storm-relative helicity changes.

Start with How do you combine positive and negative helicity contributions?.

Set up the experiment

Hold the bottom and top winds at (0,10) and (10,10) m/s. Compare assumed storm motion (0,0) with (0,5) m/s. Predict which produces the larger positive segment contribution. Subtract each storm vector from both endpoints. Evaluating u₂v₁−u₁v₂ gives +100 and +50 m²/s². This one segment represents the chosen layer in the experiment.

Practice questions

  1. Use motion (0,10) m/s.
  2. Use motion (0,15) m/s.
  3. Why can the relative value change without a change in ground-relative shear?

Worked answers

Answer 1

The lower relative wind becomes zero and the segment contributes zero.

Answer 2

The relative v component is −5 m/s at both ends. The result is −50 m²/s².

Answer 3

The wind difference still uses the same endpoints. The signed helicity also depends on the endpoints’ positions relative to storm motion.
