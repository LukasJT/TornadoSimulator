Velocity folding exercises: how do you unwrap a Doppler measurement?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Physical science · waves and logarithms · November 21, 2026

A Doppler velocity outside the measurable interval wraps by multiples of twice the Nyquist velocity. This is velocity aliasing.

Start with How wide is a radar beam when it reaches a distant supercell?.

Advertisement

Worked example

Take the unambiguous interval as [−25, +25) m/s. An outward 32 m/s velocity wraps by subtracting 50 m/s, giving −18 m/s. The observed value alone is compatible with many true velocities spaced 50 m/s apart. Dealiasing can use neighboring gates, previous scans or a wind estimate.

Practice questions

  1. Wrap +32 m/s into the interval.
  2. Wrap −32 m/s into the interval.
  3. Wrap +78 m/s.
  4. Give three true values consistent with −18 m/s.
  5. A student adds 25 m/s as one fold. Correct them.
  6. What can help distinguish real inbound wind from an aliased outbound value?

Worked answers

Answer 1

32−50 = −18 m/s.

Answer 2

−32+50 = +18 m/s.

Answer 3

78−100 = −22 m/s.

Answer 4

−68, −18 and +32 m/s are three possibilities.

Answer 5

A full fold is the entire interval width, 50 m/s.

Answer 6

Examine spatial continuity, scan history and independent winds. The folded gate alone is ambiguous.
