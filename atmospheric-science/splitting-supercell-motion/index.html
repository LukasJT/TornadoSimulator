Why can a splitting supercell move away from the mean wind?

Tornado Hub · Lab · Atmospheric science · vector calculus · November 21, 2026

Supercell splits can diverge from the mean wind because the organized updrafts propagate relative to the ambient flow. A simple track experiment measures the divergence without simulating its cause.

Start with How sensitive is ideal updraft speed to a small CAPE error?.

Set up the experiment

Use a flat coordinate system with east and north positive. Both synthetic centroids start at zero and move 8 km east over the same 600 s. One moves 3 km north and the other 3 km south. Before calculating, predict their relative lateral speed. Divide each offset by 600 s to get ±5 m/s, giving a separation speed of 10 m/s and final gap of 6 km. In real storms, dynamic pressure and the evolving updraft can contribute to propagation.

Practice questions

  1. Find each centroid’s eastward velocity.
  2. Continue both vectors for another 600 s. Find final separation.
  3. If the south centroid has no northward component while the other stays at +5 m/s, what is the 600 s gap?

Worked answers

Answer 1

8,000/600 ≈ 13.33 m/s east.

Answer 2

The gap doubles to 12 km under constant motion.

Answer 3

The northward difference is now 5 m/s. The separation is 3 km because eastward displacements are equal.
