Vortex stretching practice using an updraft velocity gradient

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · vector calculus · November 21, 2026

Stretching can amplify existing vertical vorticity when upward velocity increases with height. The local stretching contribution is ζ times the vertical gradient of w.

Start with How do you estimate circulation around a rotating updraft?.

Advertisement

Worked example

With ζ = 0.020 s⁻¹ and ∂w/∂z = 0.003 s⁻¹, the local tendency is +6.0×10⁻⁵ s⁻². Multiply by 10 s for a short-step increment of +0.0006 s⁻¹ if the tendency is held fixed. Other terms can contribute in a real storm.

Practice questions

  1. Calculate the positive-vorticity tendency shown above.
  2. Find the 10-second change with the tendency held fixed.
  3. Use ζ = 0.010 s⁻¹ and a gradient of 0.004 s⁻¹ for 20 s.
  4. Reverse the sign of the gradient in the worked setup and find the new ζ after 10 s.
  5. A student labels ζ times the gradient as s⁻¹. Correct the units.
  6. What can stretching do if initial ζ is zero and other terms vanish?

Worked answers

Answer 1

0.020×0.003 = 6.0×10⁻⁵ s⁻².

Answer 2

6.0×10⁻⁵×10 = 0.0006 s⁻¹.

Answer 3

The tendency is 4×10⁻⁵ s⁻² and the change is +0.0008 s⁻¹.

Answer 4

The change is −0.0006 s⁻¹, giving 0.0194 s⁻¹.

Answer 5

Each factor is s⁻¹, so the product is s⁻². Multiplying by seconds gives the increment in s⁻¹.

Answer 6

The stretching product is zero. This term alone cannot create vertical vorticity from zero.
