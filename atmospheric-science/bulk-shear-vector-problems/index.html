Bulk wind shear vector practice for a turning wind profile

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Physical science · vectors · November 21, 2026

Compute bulk wind shear by subtracting the bottom wind vector from the top wind vector. Take the magnitude after subtracting the components.

Start with How do you convert meteorological wind direction into u and v?.

Advertisement

Worked example

For lower wind (4,2) and upper wind (16,7) m/s, the shear vector is (16−4,7−2) = (12,5) m/s. Its magnitude is √(12²+5²) = 13 m/s. State the vertical layer and use interpolated endpoint winds if needed. Interior turning matters to the hodograph and can differ between profiles with identical endpoints.

Hint for the exercises

Subtract the east and north components separately before finding the magnitude.

Practice questions

  1. Find the east component of bulk shear in the worked sample.
  2. Find the full shear magnitude in the worked sample.
  3. Use lower (3,−4) and upper (−1,8) m/s. Find the shear vector and magnitude.
  4. Wind turns from eastward 10 m/s at the bottom to northward 10 m/s at the top. Find the shear.
  5. A classmate gets zero for the turning profile because both wind speeds are 10 m/s. What did they subtract?
  6. Can an S-shaped hodograph have zero endpoint shear?

Worked answers

Answer 1

16−4 = 12 m/s eastward.

Answer 2

The vector is (12,5) m/s. Its magnitude is 13 m/s.

Answer 3

(−4,12) m/s. Magnitude √160 ≈ 12.65 m/s.

Answer 4

(0,10)−(10,0) = (−10,10) m/s, with magnitude about 14.14 m/s.

Answer 5

They subtracted scalar speeds. Subtracting vectors retains the direction change and gives nonzero shear.

Answer 6

Yes. It can return to its initial vector while changing considerably in the middle.
