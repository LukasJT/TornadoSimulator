How do you estimate a gust front’s density-current speed?

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · dynamics · November 21, 2026

An idealized cold-pool gust front can be scaled as c = Fr√(g′h). The specified Froude coefficient summarizes geometry and flow effects.

Start with Why does baroclinicity generate vorticity along storm outflow?.

Worked example

Use reduced gravity g′ = 0.20 m/s², cold-pool depth h = 500 m and Fr = 1. The product g′h = 100 m²/s² and its square root is 10 m/s. This scaling estimates propagation relative to the ambient air under the stated assumptions.

Practice questions

  1. Find the product g′h in the example.
  2. Find speed with Fr = 1.
  3. Double depth at fixed reduced gravity.
  4. Keep the original depth and set Fr to 0.8.
  5. A student reports 100 m/s. What step was skipped?
  6. Can a measured ground-relative gust-front speed be used directly to infer depth?

Worked answers

Answer 1

It is 100 m²/s².

Answer 2

√100 = 10 m/s.

Answer 3

The speed becomes 10√2 ≈ 14.14 m/s.

Answer 4

The resulting speed is 8 m/s.

Answer 5

Take the square root of g′h before multiplying by the coefficient.

Answer 6

Ambient flow, terrain and the assumed coefficient can affect that speed. State or estimate their effects before inverting.
