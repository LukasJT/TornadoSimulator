Cloud liquid-water content practice from droplet size and number

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Environmental science · algebra · November 21, 2026

Cloud liquid-water content is the total mass of droplets per cubic metre of air. In a monodisperse example, multiply the number concentration by the mass of one spherical droplet.

Worked example

With radius 10 μm, number 100 cm⁻³ and liquid density 1,000 kg/m³, use radius 10⁻⁵ m and number 10⁸ m⁻³. Then LWC = Nρw(4πr³/3) ≈ 4.19×10⁻⁴ kg/m³ or 0.419 g/m³. Real clouds contain a range of radii and require summing over sizes.

Practice questions

  1. Find the mass of one 10 μm radius droplet.
  2. Find liquid-water content at 100 droplets per cm³.
  3. Halve concentration and double radius relative to the starting example.
  4. If measured diameter is 10 μm, find LWC at the original concentration.
  5. A student converts 100 cm⁻³ to 100 m⁻³. Correct the count.
  6. Can you use the mean radius directly in a polydisperse cloud?

Worked answers

Answer 1

1,000×4π(10⁻⁵)³/3 ≈ 4.19×10⁻¹² kg.

Answer 2

Multiply by 10⁸ m⁻³ for 0.419 g/m³.

Answer 3

Mass grows by 8 per droplet, giving 4×0.419 ≈ 1.68 g/m³.

Answer 4

Radius is 5 μm. The result is one-eighth of the original, about 0.0524 g/m³.

Answer 5

A cubic metre contains a million cubic centimetres, giving 10⁸ m⁻³.

Answer 6

Average droplet volume depends on the mean of radius cubed. Cubing the mean radius can give a different value.
