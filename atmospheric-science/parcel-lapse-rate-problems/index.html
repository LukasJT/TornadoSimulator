Dry vs. moist adiabatic lapse-rate practice problems

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · algebra · November 21, 2026

Use the dry adiabatic rate up to saturation and a moist rate for the saturated part of ascent. A specified constant moist rate approximates the actual varying rate.

Start with Why does relative humidity change when a parcel warms?.

Advertisement

Worked example

In the example, start at 25°C. Cool by 9.8 K over the first kilometre, then by 6 K/km over two saturated kilometres. The final temperature is 3.2°C. Condensation releases latent heat, so saturated ascent generally cools more slowly than dry ascent.

Practice questions

  1. Find the parcel temperature at saturation in the example.
  2. Find its final temperature.
  3. Repeat with 0.5 km dry followed by 1.5 km moist.
  4. Compare the worked profile with 3 km of all-dry ascent.
  5. A student multiplies 9.8 K/km by a rise of 500 m directly. Correct it.
  6. Can you assume the moist rate is 6 K/km in any storm?

Worked answers

Answer 1

25−9.8 = 15.2°C.

Answer 2

15.2−(2×6) = 3.2°C.

Answer 3

25−4.9−9 = 11.1°C.

Answer 4

All dry gives 25−29.4 = −4.4°C, or 7.6°C colder.

Answer 5

500 m equals 0.5 km. The cooling is 4.9 K.

Answer 6

No. It varies with the parcel’s thermodynamic state. Here the value is a supplied approximation.
