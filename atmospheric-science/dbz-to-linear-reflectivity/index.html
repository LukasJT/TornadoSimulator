How do you convert radar reflectivity from dBZ to linear Z?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Physical science · waves and logarithms · November 21, 2026

A difference of 10 dBZ corresponds to a factor of ten in linear radar reflectivity Z. Convert logarithmic values before taking ratios or averaging the underlying reflectivity.

Worked example

By convention dBZ = 10 log₁₀(Z/Z₀), where Z₀ = 1 mm⁶/m³. Subtracting dBZ values gives 10 log₁₀ of the ratio. A 45 dBZ sample relative to a 35 dBZ sample has ratio 10^((45−35)/10) = 10. Reflectivity alone does not establish a rainfall-rate ratio.

Practice questions

  1. What ratio corresponds to 3 dB?
  2. If sample B is 15 dB weaker than sample A, find B divided by A in linear units.

Worked answers

Answer 1

10^0.3 ≈ 2.00.

Answer 2

10^(−1.5) ≈ 0.0316.
