How wide is a radar beam when it reaches a distant supercell?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Physical science · waves and logarithms · November 21, 2026

Radar beam width increases with range. For a narrow beam, multiply range by beamwidth in radians to estimate its transverse footprint.

Start with How high is the radar beam above a storm at 100 km?.

Advertisement

Worked example

A one-degree beamwidth is π/180 radians. At 100 km, the width is about 1.75 km. This is the width between nominal beam boundaries, not the gate spacing along the beam. Features smaller than the footprint can be blended together.

Practice questions

  1. What is the footprint at 150 km?
  2. At what range does a 1° beam span about one kilometre?

Worked answers

Answer 1

150π/180 ≈ 2.62 km.

Answer 2

1/(π/180) ≈ 57.3 km.
