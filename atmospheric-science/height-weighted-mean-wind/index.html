How do you calculate mean wind through an unevenly sampled layer?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Physical science · vectors · November 21, 2026

Estimate a height-mean wind by averaging each pair of adjacent wind measurements, multiplying by that interval’s thickness and dividing the sum by the total depth. Do this separately for u and v.

Worked example

Assume the wind varies linearly between observations. With u values 0, 10 and 10 m/s at 0, 1 and 3 km, the first interval contributes 5 × 1 and the second 10 × 2 in units of m/s times km. Divide 25 by 3 km to find 8.33 m/s. The average of the three point samples is 6.67 m/s because it implicitly weights all measurements equally.

Practice questions

  1. Insert a sample of 10 m/s at 2 km. Does the height-weighted result change?
  2. Suppose the middle measurement was made at 2 km instead, with the same u values. Recalculate.

Worked answers

Answer 1

No. It divides a constant 2 km interval into two identical 1 km contributions.

Answer 2

(5×2 + 10×1)/3 = 20/3 ≈ 6.67 m/s.
