How do you read a reliability diagram for storm probabilities?

Tornado Hub · Lesson · Applied math · probability and statistics · November 21, 2026

A reliability diagram plots the average forecast probability on the horizontal axis and the observed frequency on the vertical axis. A point above the diagonal indicates the event occurred more often than the forecast probability in that bin.

Worked example

Group forecasts into probability bins and compute the average prediction and observed event fraction inside each bin. Record bin counts because a point built from a few cases can move a lot when one case changes. In our synthetic 30% group, 9 of 20 windows verify, giving observed frequency 0.45 and calibration point (0.30, 0.45). The observed frequency exceeds the forecast mean by 0.15, or 15 percentage points.

Practice questions

  1. What is the observed frequency for the 70% bin? How should you interpret it?
  2. One event classification changes from yes to no in each of the 10% and 70% bins. Which point moves more?

Worked answers

Answer 1

2/4 = 0.50. The sample point lies below the diagonal, with only four cases supporting it.

Answer 2

The 10% group moves by 1/50 = 0.02. The 70% group moves by 1/4 = 0.25. Keep sample sizes visible.
