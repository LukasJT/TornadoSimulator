How does the Bunkers method estimate right-moving supercell motion?

Tornado Hub · Lab · Physical science · vectors · November 21, 2026

The Bunkers method places a right-moving supercell on the right side of the deep shear vector by adding a perpendicular offset to a deep-layer mean wind. The reference offset magnitude is 7.5 m/s.

Set up the experiment

Use a surface-to-6 km mean wind and the shear from the mean surface-to-0.5 km winds to the mean 5.5-to-6 km winds. For this synthetic experiment, prescribe those means before calculating. Let the deep mean be (10,5) m/s and shear point due east. Predict which component will change. Eastward shear has a southward right side, so add (0,−7.5) for right-mover (10,−2.5) m/s. A left mover adds the opposite offset. This method estimates motion from a profile. Any observed storm can deviate from that estimate.

Practice questions

  1. Keep the mean wind fixed and rotate the shear vector due north. Find the right mover.
  2. With eastward shear, what is the vector difference between the right and left movers?
  3. What happens if the prescribed shear vector has zero magnitude?

Worked answers

Answer 1

Right of north is east. Add (7.5,0) to get (17.5,5) m/s.

Answer 2

Right minus left is (0,−15) m/s. The offsets have equal size and opposite directions.

Answer 3

Its perpendicular unit vector is undefined. The shear-relative offset cannot be constructed from that vector.
