Streamwise vs. crosswise vorticity: vector projection problems

Tornado Hub · Practice set · Atmospheric science · vector calculus · November 21, 2026

Streamwise vorticity is the projection of horizontal vorticity onto the direction the air moves in the reference frame you choose. Crosswise vorticity lies perpendicular to that direction.

Worked example

For storm-relative flow due east, the unit vector is (1,0). With horizontal vorticity (0.006,0.008) s⁻¹, the signed streamwise component is 0.006 s⁻¹ and the residual (0,0.008) s⁻¹ has magnitude 0.008 s⁻¹. In general subtract the streamwise projection vector before finding the residual magnitude.

Practice questions

  1. Find the streamwise component in the table.
  2. Find the crosswise magnitude in the table.
  3. Rotate the flow due north. Find the new streamwise component and crosswise magnitude.
  4. Let the unit flow vector be (0.6,0.8). What remains crosswise?
  5. A student projects directly onto a wind velocity in m/s. Fix their calculation.
  6. What happens to the signed streamwise component when flow reverses?

Worked answers

Answer 1

The dot product gives +0.006 s⁻¹.

Answer 2

The residual is (0,0.008) s⁻¹ and its magnitude is 0.008 s⁻¹.

Answer 3

They are +0.008 and 0.006 s⁻¹, respectively.

Answer 4

The vorticity is parallel to that vector. Its streamwise component is 0.010 s⁻¹ and the residual is zero.

Answer 5

Normalize the velocity first. Projection onto a dimensionless unit vector keeps units of s⁻¹.

Answer 6

It changes sign. The physical crosswise residual remains the same.
