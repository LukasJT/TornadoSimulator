The Day After Tomorrow
2004

Multiple simultaneous tornadoes destroy Los Angeles. Roland Emmerich stuffs every extreme weather event into 24 hours. The Sunset Strip tornado shot is genuinely gorgeous CGI, but the atmospheric setup that produces it is nonsense.

Verdict: Visually impressive, meteorologically absurd. ★★
Storm Chasers (TV series)
2007-2012

Discovery Channel. Real chasers, real chases, real footage. Some artificial drama in the editing but the storms are absolutely real — including some of the best-photographed events of the decade.

Verdict: Non-fiction reference. ★★★★★
Tornado Alley (IMAX documentary)
2011

Sean Casey's decade of chasing captured on IMAX. Some of the best real tornado footage ever recorded. Includes the 2004 Manchester, SD tornado and multiple 2010 Plains events.

Verdict: Actual reference footage. ★★★★★
Category 6: Day of Destruction
2004 (TV)

Multiple F5 tornadoes hit Chicago during a heatwave. The made-up "Category 6" is technically nonsense (the EF scale caps at 5), but the tornado effects are decent for TV.

Verdict: Fun trash. ★★
Wind (Twister prequel graphic novel)
2024

Companion piece to Twisters (2024). Interesting for its explanation of TOTO and DOW research context.

Verdict: Serious background material. ★★★★
Iron Sky: The Coming Race
2019

Multiple tornadoes appear as briefly-glimpsed disasters in the film's chaotic third act. Barely count.

Verdict: Blink and miss. ★
Wonka
2023

A blizzard, not a tornado — but the extreme wind sequences are Timothée Chalamet territory more than meteorology.

Verdict: Not really a tornado movie. —
Twisters vs. Twister
Bonus round

Which is more accurate? Twisters (2024) has better structure and radar, thanks to consulting real meteorologists. Twister (1996) has better chase dynamics — the chase feel, the road strategy, the community of chasers. Neither depicts a real tornado end-to-end faithfully.

Verdict: Twisters wins on physics, Twister wins on culture.
