Tornadoes in Joplin: History, Risk & Safety

On 22 May 2011, an EF5 tornado tore through the heart of Joplin, killing 158 people and becoming the deadliest single US tornado in more than sixty years. Here is the city's tornado history, risk, and safety picture.

The tornado risk in Joplin

Joplin sits in southwest Missouri, where the southern Great Plains and the more storm-prone Ozarks meet. Spring brings the collision of warm, moist Gulf air with cooler air aloft and strong wind shear — the classic supercell recipe. The city's location makes violent, long-track tornadoes a genuine, if infrequent, threat, and the 2011 disaster showed how catastrophic a direct hit on a populated area can be.

Notable tornadoes in Joplin history

22 May 2011 — the EF5 disaster

A large, rain-wrapped EF5 tornado moved directly through Joplin during the early evening. With peak winds estimated above 200 mph, it carved a path roughly a kilometre wide across the city, destroying thousands of homes and businesses, a hospital, and schools. The death toll of 158 made it the deadliest single tornado in the United States since modern records began in 1950, and one of the costliest ever, with damages around 2.8 billion dollars. The tragedy reshaped national thinking on tornado warnings, hospital design, and public response.

A recurring regional threat

Joplin and the surrounding Four States region have seen numerous tornadoes over the decades, most far weaker than the 2011 storm. The event was a devastating outlier, but it sits within a long record of spring severe weather across southwest Missouri, southeast Kansas, northeast Oklahoma, and northwest Arkansas.

How tornado warnings reach Joplin

Warnings for the Joplin area are issued by the local National Weather Service forecast office and pushed through Wireless Emergency Alerts to mobile phones, NOAA Weather Radio, local TV and radio, and outdoor sirens where they exist. A tornado watch means conditions are favourable across a broad region; a tornado warning means one is imminent or occurring nearby and you should shelter immediately. Learn the difference on our watch versus warning guide.

Staying safe in Joplin

Know your safe place before the season starts: a basement or storm shelter is best, otherwise a small interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows. If you live in a mobile home, identify a sturdier building nearby, because manufactured homes offer little protection. Keep a NOAA Weather Radio or phone alerts on during severe-weather setups, and never try to outrun a tornado in a vehicle in congested areas. See the full tornado safety guide.

Joplin in the bigger picture

Joplin became a case study in tornado science and emergency management. Research after the storm examined why the death toll was so high — including the rain-wrapped nature of the tornado, warning fatigue, and the short time between the siren and impact — and drove improvements in warning language and building practices.
