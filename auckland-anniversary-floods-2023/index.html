The Auckland Anniversary Floods of 2023

Extreme rainfall flooding · Auckland, New Zealand · January 27-29, 2023 · 4 killed

On January 27, 2023 — the Auckland Anniversary long weekend — record-breaking rainfall flooded Auckland, New Zealand's largest city, in what became the city's wettest day on record. The flooding killed 4 people and forced a state of emergency, just weeks before Cyclone Gabrielle struck the wider region.

What Happened

An unusually moist and slow-moving weather system stalled over the greater Auckland region on the Anniversary Weekend, dumping extraordinary rainfall totals within a matter of hours. Some parts of the city recorded well over 200mm of rain in around 24 hours — a total that overwhelmed stormwater infrastructure never designed for that intensity of rainfall in a single day.

Flash flooding developed rapidly across low-lying parts of the city, trapping motorists in flooded streets and inundating homes and businesses with little warning as water levels rose faster than many residents could react to.

Damage and Impact

Roads across Auckland became impassable rivers within hours, Auckland Airport's terminal flooded and briefly closed, and numerous homes and businesses across the city sustained significant water damage. Four people died in flooding-related incidents, and the scale of the disruption prompted the declaration of a local state of emergency for the Auckland region.

  • 4 fatalities across the Auckland region
  • Auckland's wettest day on record, with well over 200mm in some areas
  • Auckland Airport terminal flooded and temporarily closed
  • Widespread road closures and property damage across the city

A City Hit Twice in Weeks

The Auckland floods proved to be a grim preview of what was still to come: just over two weeks later, Cyclone Gabrielle struck the North Island, further battering an already-stretched Auckland region and devastating Hawke's Bay and Gisborne further south. The back-to-back disasters meant emergency services, infrastructure repair crews, and insurers across the North Island faced two major flooding events in rapid succession rather than the recovery window that usually follows a single disaster.

Why Auckland Flooded So Fast

Auckland's mix of hilly terrain, extensive urban development, and stormwater systems built for historical rainfall patterns rather than the increasingly intense rain events being recorded made the city particularly vulnerable to flash flooding once rainfall intensity exceeded what its drainage network could handle. The event prompted renewed scrutiny of Auckland's stormwater infrastructure and its capacity to handle increasingly extreme rainfall.

Aftermath

Recovery and infrastructure repair in Auckland were still underway when Cyclone Gabrielle arrived weeks later, complicating the region's response. Together, the two events prompted a significant, ongoing reassessment of Auckland's flood resilience, insurance availability in flood-prone suburbs, and long-term land-use planning in low-lying parts of the city.
