Tornado Emergency Kit - Complete Supply List

A well-stocked tornado emergency kit can be the difference between minor inconvenience and catastrophic loss. Here's the definitive 2026 checklist covering what to include, why each item matters, and where to source everything. Kit-building is one of the most important tornado preparation steps.

The Essential Kit Categories

A complete tornado emergency kit covers:

  • Water and food
  • First aid
  • Light and communication
  • Personal documents and cash
  • Clothing and shelter
  • Tools and safety equipment
  • Sanitation supplies
  • Special needs items

Water Supply

How Much Water

Minimum requirements:

  • 1 gallon per person per day
  • 3-day supply minimum
  • 7-day supply preferred
  • Additional for pets
  • Extra for cooking/hygiene

Water Storage

  • Sealed water containers
  • Store in cool, dark location
  • Rotate every 6-12 months
  • Label with date
  • Consider bottled water for emergency

Food Supply

Non-Perishable Options

  • Canned goods (with manual can opener)
  • Peanut butter
  • Crackers
  • Granola bars
  • Trail mix
  • Dried fruits
  • Cereal
  • Rice
  • Pasta

Emergency Meal Kits

Convenient options:

  • MREs (Meal Ready to Eat)
  • Freeze-dried meals
  • Long-shelf-life meal kits
  • Emergency food rations

Special Considerations

  • Consider dietary restrictions
  • Include comfort foods
  • Children's preferences
  • Elderly needs

First Aid Supplies

Basic Kit Components

  • Adhesive bandages (multiple sizes)
  • Sterile gauze pads
  • Medical tape
  • Antiseptic wipes
  • Antibiotic ointment
  • Ibuprofen/acetaminophen
  • Prescription medications (7-day supply)
  • Digital thermometer
  • Latex gloves
  • Sterile scissors
  • Tweezers

Advanced First Aid

  • Israeli combat bandage
  • Tourniquet
  • Instant cold packs
  • Emergency blanket
  • CPR mouth barrier
  • Splint materials

Light and Communication

Lighting

  • LED flashlight (multiple)
  • Headlamp (hands-free)
  • Chemical light sticks
  • Battery-powered lantern
  • Emergency candles + matches

Communication

  • NOAA weather radio
  • Battery-powered radio
  • Extra batteries (multiple sizes)
  • Cell phone charger
  • External battery bank
  • Hand-crank radio
  • Solar charger (optional)

NOAA Weather Radio Recommendation

  • Automatic tornado alerts
  • SAME technology
  • Reliable operation
  • Reasonable price

Personal Documents

Physical Documents

Include copies:

  • Driver's license
  • Passport
  • Insurance policies
  • Medical records
  • Bank account information
  • Vehicle titles
  • Property deeds
  • Marriage/birth certificates

Digital Backups

Store digitally:

  • Cloud storage
  • USB drive
  • Email backup
  • Password-protected

Emergency Contact List

Include:

  • Family contacts
  • Out-of-state relative
  • Insurance company
  • Bank contacts
  • Employer
  • Local emergency services

Cash

Emergency Cash

  • $200-$500 minimum
  • Small bills preferred
  • Quarters for parking meters/laundry
  • Keep in waterproof container
  • ATMs may be inoperable after disaster

Clothing and Shelter

Change of Clothes

  • Rugged pants/shorts
  • Long-sleeved shirt
  • Sturdy shoes/boots
  • Socks
  • Weather-appropriate outer layer
  • Weather-appropriate hat
  • Rain gear

Shelter Materials

  • Emergency blanket (Mylar)
  • Sleeping bag
  • Waterproof tarp
  • Duct tape
  • Plastic sheeting

Tools and Safety

Basic Tools

  • Crowbar
  • Adjustable wrench
  • Screwdriver set
  • Utility knife
  • Wire cutters
  • Duct tape
  • Pliers
  • Hammer
  • Waterproof matches
  • Lighter

Safety Equipment

  • Fire extinguisher
  • Whistle
  • Dust masks
  • Work gloves
  • Safety glasses

Sanitation Supplies

Basic Sanitation

  • Toilet paper
  • Feminine hygiene products
  • Toothbrush and toothpaste
  • Soap
  • Wet wipes
  • Antibacterial wipes
  • Trash bags
  • Hand sanitizer

Water for Sanitation

  • Extra water for washing
  • Water for teeth brushing
  • Water for cleaning wounds

Elderly and Disabled

  • Prescription medications
  • Medical equipment
  • Backup batteries for equipment
  • Contact lens supplies
  • Hearing aid batteries
  • Cane/walker
  • Special dietary needs

Infants and Children

  • Formula
  • Diapers
  • Baby food
  • Comfort items
  • Special toys
  • Coloring books
  • Stuffed animals

Pets

Kit Storage

Location

Store where:

  • Easily accessible
  • Near shelter location
  • Waterproof
  • Not exposed to elements
  • Regular access for maintenance

Multiple Locations

  • Home main kit
  • Bedroom quick-grab kit
  • Vehicle basic kit
  • Work location kit
  • Family designated meeting places

Maintenance

Monthly Checks

  • Test flashlight batteries
  • Check first aid supplies
  • Verify NOAA radio function
  • Update cash amount

6-Month Rotation

  • Rotate water supply
  • Check food expiration
  • Replace batteries
  • Update contact information
  • Refresh medications

Annual Review

  • Comprehensive review
  • Update all documents
  • Replace expired items
  • Add any new needs
  • Practice using kit

Build Your Own

Estimated Cost

Basic Kit (3-day)

  • Food/water: $50-$100
  • First aid: $30-$75
  • Communication: $50-$100
  • Miscellaneous: $30-$50
  • Total: $160-$325

Enhanced Kit (7-day)

  • Basic multiplied
  • Additional supplies
  • Better tools
  • Total: $400-$800

Premium Kit (Extended)

  • Comprehensive supplies
  • High-quality items
  • Backup equipment
  • Total: $1,000-$2,500

Bottom Line

A well-stocked tornado emergency kit is essential preparation. Build gradually - start with basics and add over time. Focus on water, food, first aid, communication, and shelter. Rotate supplies and maintain equipment regularly. A $200-500 investment can protect your family during and after tornado disasters. Don't wait until warning day to build your kit.
